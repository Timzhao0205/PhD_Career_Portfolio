# P5 red team G02 — independent adversarial review

Scope: `P3R2-C-22`, `P3R2-C-01`, `P3R2-B-01`, `P3R2-D-19`, and `P3R2-F-12`. This review tries to kill each idea under the frozen 2030-launch assumptions. It does not edit the authoritative P4 scores. Score changes below are adjudication suggestions, not replacements.

## P3R2-C-22 — electrolyzer degradation-emulation and bankability test systems

**Strongest bear case.** The record proves spending on electrolyzers, not product-specific demand for a merchant degradation-emulation bench or a shared bankability dataset. DOE hub money is phase-gated: DOE's own funding notice says later phases depend on Go/No-Go reviews of earlier deliverables, so a headline hub award is not a procurement commitment for this product ([DOE OCED funding notice](https://oced-exchange.energy.gov/Default.aspx?Search=hydrogen+hubs&SearchType=)). The proposed anti-fragility is overstated: a contracting market may increase scrutiny per surviving project but also removes OEMs, projects, and budgets. No cited OEM, developer, lender, insurer, or Chinese EPC has bought this service or stated that it will share field-failure data across OEMs.

The hardware wedge is also substantially narrower than P4 states. Greenlight has built electrolyzer test rigs since 1996, offers automated dynamic testing and power supplies through the multi-megawatt range, and its current high-power supply specifies configurable 100 kW–5 MW outputs and <=1% full-scale voltage ripple ([Greenlight E100](https://www.greenlightinnovation.com/products/electrolyser/electrolyser-testing/e100-electrolyser-test-station), [Greenlight high-power fact sheet](https://www.greenlightinnovation.com/files/file/67d0d2469f828/ETS-Power-Supply-Fact-Sheet.pdf)). Thus programmable profiles and clean/ripple-controlled power are not themselves whitespace. The remaining product is an inference model plus shared data. That is the hardest asset to form because OEMs have warranty liability and strong incentives not to pool identifiable degradation data. Accelerating “20 years” into 90 days is not a 10x edge until it predicts held-out field outcomes across stack chemistries; otherwise it is a faster but potentially misleading lab protocol.

Incumbent response is cheap: Greenlight can add profile libraries and analytics to an installed test-platform relationship; OEMs can retain their existing in-house benches and data. V1 capital of $4–10M is partly above the founder's preferred $5M cumulative line, while electrochemistry interpretation, safety systems, gas handling, calibration, and global field service make this more than a programmable-power build. US and China both have category activity, but neither independent leg currently clears willingness-to-pay for this exact product. Export controls are manageable, though separate US/CN data governance and customer confidentiality can prevent the very cross-OEM dataset that creates the moat. The 2030 China target and surviving US hubs keep a testing window open; they do not guarantee that an independent dataset vendor wins it.

**Disconfirming evidence.** `P3R2-C-22-S02` and Greenlight's current product materials disconfirm the hardware-vacuum premise. `P3R2-C-22-S03` shows that two of seven US hubs were already cancelled. `L11-047` and `L11-048` show Western OEM order-book contraction. The EU JRC source supports a standards gap but calls its work proposed protocols and prototype-qualification guidance, not a mandate for third-party commercial testing ([EU JRC record](https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/handle/JRC133726)). These facts make the data-sharing and paid-validation assumptions load-bearing.

**Steelman.** This is still the strongest concept in the group on founder-scale technical controllability. Ripple, open-circuit intervals, impedance, crossover, efficiency, and degradation signatures are measurable. The US and China demand legs are structurally separable. A credible third-party correlation dataset could lower warranty reserves or financing risk, and both boom and consolidation can create qualification work. The bench is useful even if the grand cross-OEM data platform fails.

**Kill probability: 60%.** The decisive risk is not physics; it is obtaining paid, permissioned field data and proving out-of-sample life prediction before Greenlight or OEM labs add the feature.

**Hard-gate verdicts.** G1 **FAIL pending repair**: there are multiple category-demand sources, but no primary buyer/procurement/filing for a merchant degradation-emulation or bankability product; the P4 flip condition remains unmet. G2 **PASS**: cited mechanism papers are accepted peer-reviewed works and the standards-gap evidence is official. G3 **PASS**: the 50 kW/two-stack/90-day campaign is bounded, explicit, and budgeted at $250k. G4 **PASS MARGINAL**: Greenlight is a direct incumbent; the cross-OEM predictive dataset is non-cosmetic but unproven and easy for customers to withhold. G5 **PASS** only because the experiment can falsify acceleration fidelity rather than assuming it. G6 **PASS MARGINAL**: no structural export block, but CN/US confidentiality and data-localization arrangements can block the shared-data moat. G7 **PASS**: US commissioning and China's 2030 hydrogen target support a launch window, with contraction risk acknowledged.

**Score-adjustment suggestion.** Reduce demonstrated demand 3->2 (-3.2), high-end niche quality 4->3 (-2.0), competition whitespace 3->2 (-1.8), and 10x edge 3->2 (-1.4). Illustrative total: **64.6**, down from 73.0, before the hard-gate penalty. Do not count US+China as a 5/5 until each leg yields a product-specific paid evaluation.

**Cheapest decisive falsification experiment.** A genuinely decisive sub-$100k commercial/scientific sub-test is possible: cap at **$95k over six months**; use two 1–5 kW commercial short stacks, a leased or partner-hosted programmable supply, pre-register three stress signatures, and predict a held-out validation segment. In parallel, require one OEM or EPC and one lender/warranty counterparty to pay at least $10k each and grant permission to use anonymized field comparators. Kill if held-out mechanism classification is below 80%, life-ranking reverses between training and held-out stacks, or either payer class refuses a paid/permissioned pilot. This does not validate 20-year life, but it decisively tests the two load-bearing premises: predictive transfer and paid data access.

**Final verdict: HOLD.** Promote only after G1 is repaired by a paid OEM/developer/financier evaluation and the cheap held-out test passes.

## P3R2-C-01 — 800 VDC rack power-path protection shelf

**Strongest bear case.** The demand is real, but a 2030 startup may arrive after the product boundary has been absorbed into standards and incumbent shelves. NVIDIA names fault detection and serviceability as an innovation area, but it also places overcurrent devices at several system boundaries and coordinates a broad MGX ecosystem ([NVIDIA 800 VDC architecture](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-800-v-hvdc-architecture-will-power-the-next-generation-of-ai-factories/)). OCP's March 2026 Diablo 400 v0.7.0 already specifies grounding options, fault behavior, and a fuse-based protection alternative rather than reserving a merchant integrated shelf socket ([OCP Diablo 400 v0.7.0](https://www.opencompute.org/documents/ocp-specification-diablo-400-v0-7-0-final-pdf)). Eaton is publicly shaping OCP, UL, and IEC frameworks while selling the surrounding power architecture ([Eaton at NVIDIA GTC 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/zh-tw/on-demand/session/gtc26-ex82160/)). DG Matrix's inclusion in NVIDIA MGX, ABB SACE Infinitus, LS Electric, Vertiv, Eaton, Delta, Huawei, and SuperX make incumbent response immediate rather than hypothetical.

Product-specific demand is stronger here than elsewhere because buyer-led specifications explicitly require protection, grounding, fault detection, and serviceability. What is unproven is willingness to buy the proposed combined SSCB+precharge+IMD+hot-swap module from a new merchant. The claimed 10x sub-100 us clearing is relative to fuses/mechanical breakers, not to emerging solid-state competitors that are also microsecond-class. Integration and nuisance-trip discrimination may be valuable, but they are not yet a measured 10x customer outcome. The $350k brassboard exceeds the preferred decisive-experiment range and the $8–20M v1 is materially above the founder's capital envelope. Certification, 10 kA–40 kA available-fault-current environments, hot-swap endurance, low-inductance packaging, field firmware, and global failure analysis create a heavier qualification/service burden than “shelf module with telemetry” suggests.

The US and China legs are independently plausible, but a common product is not automatic. China lacks a settled 800 V safety-certification framework and has domestic component/vendor ecosystems; a dual-entity structure does not erase semiconductor export licensing, IP leakage, or hyperscaler qualification barriers. By 2030, US first-wave designs will have three years of field history, while China's domestic suppliers may converge behind local standards. G7 therefore turns on a 2028–29 design-in, not merely an industry roadmap.

**Disconfirming evidence.** `P3R2-C-01-S04` documents more than $320M of startup funding and multiple certified/funded competitors. The current OCP specification's explicit fuse option disconfirms the assumption that a solid-state integrated unit is necessary. NVIDIA's architecture shows that the platform owner is already allocating protection functions. The OAII forum proves unresolved CN pain, but it is a forum statement, not a purchase order for this module.

**Steelman.** This is a genuine, buyer-defined problem with strong founder skill transfer. The integrated protection/sequencing/telemetry layer can reduce coordination failures across breakers, IMD, precharge, connectors, and firmware. If an OCP contributor or CN ODM defines an embeddable interface and agrees to qualify it, a focused startup could own the cross-vendor protection module while larger companies concentrate on full shelves.

**Kill probability: 55%.** The most likely kill is spec absorption or incumbent qualification before the startup exists, not a physics failure.

**Hard-gate verdicts.** G1 **PASS**: NVIDIA plus buyer-led OCP specifications independently document the exact protection/serviceability job, though a merchant purchase remains unproven. G2 **PASS**: the SSCB and arc-detection base is peer-review-cleared. G3 **PASS MARGINAL**: the 6–18 month brassboard is explicit but $350k is above the preferred band and the sellable v1 requires $8–20M. G4 **PASS MARGINAL**: competitors are named and integration is non-cosmetic, but the merchant socket can be absorbed into OCP/MGX or incumbent shelves. G5 **PASS**: established physics and staged testing. G6 **PASS MARGINAL**: no structural prohibition, but gallium licensing, separate BOMs, certification, and CN entity/IP controls must be base-case costs. G7 **PASS MARGINAL**: the 2030 window survives only with a 2028–29 design-in; otherwise first-wave qualification locks the entrant out.

**Score-adjustment suggestion.** Reduce competition whitespace 2->1 (-1.8), reachable validation budget 3->2 (-1.8), 10x edge 3->2 (-1.4), and launch-window fit 3->2 (-1.6). Illustrative total: **59.6**, down from 66.2.

**Cheapest decisive falsification experiment.** No sub-$100k test can honestly validate interruption at 800 V/250–500 A, the target fault energy, 10,000 hot swaps, and certification readiness. A **$45k, four-month commercial-boundary sub-test** is nonetheless decisive for the merchant-socket thesis: obtain confidential v0.7.0 requirement redlines from two OCP power integrators and one CN ODM; require at least one written evaluation agreement that assigns the combined protection/sequencing function to a replaceable third-party module through 2029. Kill if every counterparty allocates the function to its own shelf, fuse, or platform-standard design. Do not count this as the decisive physics experiment or as satisfaction of the portfolio's cheap-technical-experiment quota.

**Final verdict: HOLD.** Retain as a high-upside option only if the merchant socket is contractually visible by 2029; otherwise kill as late and capital-heavy.

## P3R2-B-01 — negative-pressure two-phase cold-plate loop

**Strongest bear case.** P4's key whitespace claim is already stale. Envicool announced a pumped two-phase cold-plate liquid-cooling system in November 2025, integrating refrigerant pump, CDU, condenser, and reservoir, and says it is backed by more than 2 GW of deployed liquid-cooling capacity ([Envicool pumped two-phase cold plate](https://www.envicool.com/en/index.php/newsinfo2459.html)). This is not merely Sugon's immersion architecture; it is the same two-phase cold-plate product class sold by the incumbent that the idea hoped to license to. Envicool also publishes shipping single-phase cold plates rated to 1.2 kW/GPU and 1.5 kW/ASIC and a server solution exceeding 2 kW total heat removal ([Envicool product brochure](https://en.envicool.com/upload/file/20250928/1759043239546830.pdf), [Envicool full-area server](https://www.envicool.com/en/index.php/newsinfo2460.html)). The negative-pressure/inward-leak feature can still differ, but the forcing function “two phase is unavailable domestically” is false.

Product-specific demand remains absent. NDRC PUE caps, the SAMR revision, and a liquid-cooling tender prove category spend, not a buyer requirement for subatmospheric two-phase operation. The strongest 10x claim is binary leak direction, but no buyer evidence assigns an order-of-magnitude economic value to it; the thermal target is only >=30% lower resistance. Negative pressure also creates hidden penalties: large vapor volume, noncondensable-gas accumulation, vacuum maintenance, blind-mate seal integrity, and service technicians trained on a nonstandard loop. An Envicool-class incumbent can respond by adding negative-pressure control to its already-integrated pumped two-phase system, while a foreign founder's licensing partner would receive the process know-how needed to copy it.

The $250k experiment barely fits the upper budget boundary and does not include rack-scale noncondensable management or service-cycle qualification. A $3–8M v1 may fit only through licensing, the route that most weakens IP control. There is no US leg. The CN route is legally possible but commercially dependent on a domestic incumbent/ODM. Export controls are low, yet IP, local certification, fluid supply, and after-sales service dominate. The 2030 PUE window persists, but the competitive window for a two-phase cold-plate entrant opened before 2026 and is closing now.

**Disconfirming evidence.** The direct Envicool announcement disconfirms the “no domestic two-phase cold-plate product” premise. `P3R2-B-01-S03` expressly does not specify two-phase or negative pressure. `P3R2-B-01-S05` shows a second domestic phase-change architecture at megawatt scale. The ITRI low-pressure result supports physics but also identifies an established regional technical rival.

**Steelman.** A water/low-GWP, subatmospheric loop could still beat positive-pressure refrigerant systems on leak consequence, fluid availability, and operating cost. Vacuum decay is measurable, and PUE rules plus rising heat flux provide a real category tailwind. A narrow component/IP license around inward-leak manifolds and noncondensable control may survive even if the full CDU is not startup whitespace.

**Kill probability: 78%.** The combination of no mechanism-specific buyer, an exact-class domestic incumbent, a CN-only partner route, and weak 10x evidence makes survival unlikely.

**Hard-gate verdicts.** G1 **FAIL**: multiple sources establish liquid-cooling demand, but none is a primary buyer/procurement/filing for negative-pressure two-phase cold plates. G2 **PASS**: technical claims rest on eligible literature/official technical evidence. G3 **PASS MARGINAL**: the $250k side-by-side experiment is bounded, but it does not cover rack/service failure modes. G4 **PASS MARGINAL**: Envicool is now an exact-class pumped two-phase competitor; negative pressure is non-cosmetic but not buyer-validated. G5 **PASS**: physics exists and the test is falsifiable. G6 **PASS MARGINAL**: low export-control risk, high licensing/IP and service dependence. G7 **PASS MARGINAL**: PUE demand persists through 2030, but domestic productization is already occurring well before launch.

**Score-adjustment suggestion.** Reduce demonstrated demand 3->2 (-3.2), competition whitespace 3->1 (-3.6), 10x edge 3->2 (-1.4), US–China leverage 2->1 (-2.0), and launch-window fit 3->2 (-1.6). Illustrative total: **51.0**, down from 62.8, before the failed-gate penalty.

**Cheapest decisive falsification experiment.** A **$55k, four-month buyer-and-teardown sub-test** can decisively test the residual wedge: acquire detailed Envicool pumped-two-phase technical/quotation material through a qualified integration partner; perform a function-by-function comparison; and obtain written requirements from three CN thermal integrators/operators. Continue only if at least one buyer states that inward-leak behavior is a mandatory paid criterion unmet by Envicool and agrees to a paid evaluation. Kill if the incumbent already controls subatmospheric pressure, can add it without redesign, or no buyer prices the leak-direction feature. This is decisive for the commercial wedge, not a substitute for the $250k thermal/reliability experiment.

**Final verdict: KILL.** Reopen only on a paid buyer specification for negative pressure, not on general liquid-cooling growth.

## P3R2-D-19 — distributed composite-flywheel buffers for off-grid AI power plants

**Strongest bear case.** The flagship order validates Piller, not an open startup socket. Piller now markets SHIELDX specifically for AI data centers, claims 50 MW–1 GW+ scale, containerized deployment, and shipment in four to six weeks ([Piller SHIELDX](https://www.piller.com/product/shieldx-dynamic-power-stabilisation/)). The proposed startup's composite rotor and DC coupling do not show a 10x customer advantage over this incumbent's proven electromechanical stabilizer plus bidirectional kinetic modules. Stornetic supplies a close composite-flywheel analog. Batteries, supercapacitors, generator controls, rack capacitors, and software all attack different parts of the transient spectrum.

The most damaging refresh is upstream smoothing. NVIDIA's June 2026 Vera Rubin material says Intelligent Power Smoothing cushions synchronized workload swings ([NVIDIA COMPUTEX 2026](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-gtc-taipei-computex-2026-news/)). Delta's GTC 2026 session shows a 110 kW shelf with 1,200 J of buffering and says its control makes input dynamics nearly flat without an extra unit ([Delta at NVIDIA GTC 2026](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/on-demand/session/gtc26-s82097/)). The residual plant-level niche may remain, especially for islanded generation, but its amplitude, duration, and cycle frequency must be measured after these controls. Piller itself acknowledges rack smoothing while arguing residual multi-megawatt steps remain; that is a vendor assertion, not independent buyer sizing.

Product-specific demand consists of one supplier-announced 400 MW project using the incumbent. GE Vernova's data-center orders corroborate power buildout, not flywheel demand. The 50 kW/2 MJ rig costs $350k and the sellable v1 costs $8–20M, both outside the preferred founder path. Composite-rotor containment, vacuum and bearing fleet maintenance, site certification, field balancing, spare modules, and catastrophic-failure liability create heavy service burden. The US beachhead is real but incumbent-held; China is explicitly not a beachhead. Export controls are manageable, but occupied-facility safety and certification are not. By 2030 Piller will have five more years of AI references, while rack smoothing improves.

**Disconfirming evidence.** NVIDIA and Delta directly disconfirm the assumption that all workload transients propagate to the plant. Piller's current product page disconfirms the idea that the incumbent is an old synchronous-machine architecture waiting to be disrupted; it is already positioning modular bidirectional kinetic stabilization for exact AI workloads. `P3R2-D-19-S03` shows a composite/AMB competitor exists outside data centers.

**Steelman.** Exact-class procurement is rare and valuable evidence. Islanded engines cannot absorb every aggregate step, rack smoothing cannot cover generator trips or supply-side faults, and kinetic systems avoid battery cycle degradation. If measured residual spectra show a feeder-level band that Piller's plant-scale architecture handles inefficiently, a smaller DC-native module could become a defensible subsystem.

**Kill probability: 70%.** Strong category demand is outweighed by incumbent ownership, unmeasured residual need, no 10x edge, high v1 capital, and one-market concentration.

**Hard-gate verdicts.** G1 **PASS MARGINAL**: the Bergen/Piller contract announcement is exact-class primary company evidence and names E-Finity; GE is independent category corroboration, but there is no second independent buyer record for distributed composite modules. G2 **PASS**: no unreviewed academic dependency. G3 **PASS MARGINAL** only if the load-spectrum study is explicitly budgeted and made decisive; the frozen $350k rotor rig is above the preferred band. G4 **PASS MARGINAL**: competitors/substitutes are named, but the DC-native composite distinction lacks a customer-valued 10x result. G5 **PASS**: mature physics and a measure-before-build sequence. G6 **PASS**: no structural cross-border block; containment and occupied-site safety remain expensive qualification gates. G7 **PASS MARGINAL**: off-grid buildout persists, but 2030 residual demand depends on how much NVIDIA/PSU-level smoothing removes and Piller already ships.

**Score-adjustment suggestion.** Reduce high-end niche quality 3->2 (-2.0), competition whitespace 2->1 (-1.8), reachable validation budget 2->1 (-1.8), 10x edge 2->1 (-1.4), and launch-window fit 3->2 (-1.6). Illustrative total: **51.8**, down from 60.4.

**Cheapest decisive falsification experiment.** A genuinely decisive **$90k, six-month field study** is possible and should replace the rotor as the first experiment. Install synchronized power-quality meters at one AI rack/power shelf and its islanded or generator-test bus; record at least 1,000 representative training transitions; subtract measured NVIDIA/PSU smoothing; then price Piller, BESS+supercap, and the proposed module against the same residual spectrum. Kill if the 99.9th-percentile residual can be covered by existing shelf energy/control or if modeled lifetime cost is not at least 30% below Piller/BESS at a market large enough for 100 modules/year. No rotor should be built without this result.

**Final verdict: HOLD.** Keep only as an option on a measured residual niche; do not select for deep dive on current evidence.

## P3R2-F-12 — CCS-native marine DC protection and swap-pack interface

**Strongest bear case.** The regulatory need is real, but the proposed merchant product boundary is not. The current CCS rules cover battery systems, distribution systems, monitoring, alarms, controls, safety, containerized mobile-power inspection, shore monitoring, and online data exchange ([CCS Rules for Ships Applying Battery as a Power 2025](https://www.ccs.org.cn/ccswz/specialDetail?id=202601060114611630)). Those requirements invite integrated, certified systems from battery suppliers and ship integrators; they do not create a standalone buyer order for an external protection+IMD+interface stack.

The exact functional neighborhood is already crowded. A Fujian government/Xinhua report says Yantai Chuangwei completed 104 major and 247 sub-tests and received CCS type approval for a comprehensive marine lithium-battery safety system at the end of 2025 ([Fujian government record](https://gxt.fujian.gov.cn/zwgk/xw/hydt/xydt/202601/t20260104_7070851.htm)). Dukosi and a Chinese BMS partner announced an operating CCS-approved all-battery vessel with cell-level monitoring in May 2026 ([Dukosi/Yichuan vessel](https://www.dukosi.com/press-release/dukosi-and-yichuan-intelligent-technology-power-first-dkcms-enabled-all-battery-new-energy-vessel-in-china)). Racern publishes a CCS/DNV/LR/NK-certified marine ESS with BMS, independent hardware protection, fire suppression, cloud monitoring, and modular swap capability ([Racern Jupiter ESS](https://www.racern.com/en/jupiter-ess/)). Together with CALB, CATL, Danfoss, and CSSC-affiliated suppliers already in the record, this undermines both whitespace and 10x differentiation.

Product-specific demand is missing: no non-CATL/non-CALB shipyard procurement says protection, IMD, and pack interface will be bought separately. Incumbents can bundle these functions with cells, BMS, converters, class dossiers, and field service. The $300k appraisal/bench is above the preferred band and does not cover a vessel pilot; v1 at $3–8M understates class campaigns, shipyard customization, environmental qualification, spares, commissioning, and years of marine support. A foreign founder must also navigate a CN-only base through a partner. BIS's official affiliates rule extends Entity List restrictions to 50%-owned affiliates, making ownership diligence a binding condition for CSSC-connected routes ([BIS affiliates rule](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-expands-entity-list-cover-affiliates-listed-entities)). There is no US beachhead; DNV/ABS is an export option, not US demand. The 2030 rule window remains open, but domestic suppliers are already certified in 2025–26.

**Disconfirming evidence.** `P3R2-F-12-S05` shows CALB already holds multi-society approvals. `P3R2-F-12-S01` shows Danfoss has held CCS approval and a CSSC partnership since 2020. The newer Chuangwei, Dukosi/Yichuan, and Racern evidence disconfirms the “nascent specialist supply base” and shows the proposed monitoring/safety/interface features being bundled.

**Steelman.** CCS compliance creates recurring, non-discretionary engineering work, and the market is large enough that non-CATL integrators exist. A narrow vendor-neutral interface certification layer could help shipyards mix batteries, packs, and shore systems. The founder can prototype protection and telemetry, and class documentation can form a moat if a buyer sponsors it.

**Kill probability: 86%.** Without a standalone RFQ, this is a systems-integration feature set inside better-capitalized, already-certified battery and marine-electric offerings.

**Hard-gate verdicts.** G1 **FAIL**: official rules and fleet growth prove category demand, but no primary buyer/procurement/filing supports purchase of the standalone protection/IMD/interface product. G2 **PASS**: no unverified academic dependency. G3 **PASS MARGINAL**: the appraisal/bench is bounded but $300k and does not include full approval/pilot. G4 **FAIL**: named competitors now bundle safety, BMS, monitoring, certification, and modular pack functions; the remaining merchant difference is not demonstrated. G5 **PASS**: mature physics and no single forecast dependency. G6 **PASS MARGINAL**: class paths exist, but CSSC ownership screening and US-origin technology controls can invalidate the preferred JV. G7 **PASS**: rules are effective and demand should persist into 2030; competition, not readiness, closes the window.

**Score-adjustment suggestion.** Reduce demonstrated demand 3->2 (-3.2), high-end niche quality 3->2 (-2.0), competition whitespace 2->1 (-1.8), technical elegance/controllability 3->2 (-2.2), 10x edge 2->1 (-1.4), and launch-window fit 4->3 (-1.6). Illustrative total: **44.2**, down from 56.4, before two failed gates.

**Cheapest decisive falsification experiment.** A **$75k, five-month merchant-socket test** is genuinely decisive before hardware: commission a formal CCS concept review; map the functional scope and certificates of CALB, Chuangwei, Racern, Dukosi/Yichuan, and two ship-electrical suppliers; then require standalone RFQs or paid design-study letters from three non-integrated shipyards/system integrators. Kill unless at least two buyers specify the stack separately from batteries/BMS, one funds at least $25k of the study, and a screened non-CSSC-affiliated manufacturing partner accepts the architecture. This validates the commercial socket and compliance route; it does not replace later type approval.

**Final verdict: KILL.** Reconsider only after a buyer-funded standalone RFQ clears G1 and the competitor scope proves a real G4 difference.
