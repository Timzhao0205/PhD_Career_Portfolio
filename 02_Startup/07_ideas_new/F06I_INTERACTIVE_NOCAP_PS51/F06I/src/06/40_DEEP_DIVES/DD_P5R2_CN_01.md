## Thesis

The product is a physical-and-software retrofit that converts a qualifying installed 2D microfocus X-ray cabinet into a limited-angle computed-laminography station for flat advanced packages and substrates. It consists of a cabinet-specific tilted precision stage, fiducial and geometry-calibration artifacts, motion and acquisition control, sparse-view reconstruction, defect-review software, and a compatibility and repeatability dossier. It does **not** replace or modify the X-ray source, detector, shielding enclosure, safety interlocks, or cabinet high-voltage system.

The customer job is to separate defects that overlap in an ordinary projection: voids, cracks, bridges, layer offsets, die-attach discontinuities, and other buried features in thin, wide, multilayer structures. The attraction is not generic “AI inspection.” It is recovery of depth information from a cabinet the buyer already owns, without buying a complete premium X-ray microscopy or in-line laminography system. A startup earns a socket only if that retrofit works repeatably on at least two cabinet brands, leaves radiation safety and vendor service boundaries intact, and produces actionable results at materially lower time and cost than the buyer's available alternatives.

Demand exists for the underlying inspection job. Guangzhou Guangxin Packaging Substrate solicited an X-ray inspection machine for layer-deviation inspection [P5R2-CN-S01]. Wuxi Shennan Circuits independently solicited an online X-ray inspection machine [P5R2-CN-S02]. In the US, NIST's acquisition record specifies a ZEISS Xradia Versa XRM 730 and multi-year support, showing that a sophisticated metrology buyer pays for high-resolution 3D X-ray capability [P5R2-CN-S03]. These records do not say that any buyer wants a retrofit, and none is a purchase order, signed access agreement, or letter of intent for this company. They clear demand for the job, not product-market fit.

## Buyers, workflow, and demand mechanism

The first China counterparties are packaging-substrate plants and failure-analysis laboratories that already operate shielded X-ray cabinets. The buyer is usually the inspection or failure-analysis engineering function, but equipment engineering, radiation safety, quality, IT/security, and procurement can each veto the retrofit. The user must locate a suspected defect before committing to destructive cross-sectioning, scrapping material, or changing process settings.

The first US counterparties are advanced-packaging failure-analysis laboratories, metrology organizations, and packaging process-development teams with an installed cabinet and a recurring queue of ambiguous flat samples. The NIST procurement proves the complete-system buying route, not a startup route [P5R2-CN-S03]. That distinction matters: a laboratory that needs the highest resolution, warranty continuity, and vendor-qualified workflow may rationally buy a new complete instrument. The retrofit is for a different case—adequate source/detector performance already exists, but projection overlap prevents the installed asset from answering a valuable subset of questions.

The failure modes are economically credible. Peer-reviewed packaging literature describes continuing qualification and reliability challenges in high-temperature devices and packaging [L15-001], mechanical reliability of sintered-silver die attach [L15-005], nano-silver interfaces [L15-017], and power-cycling degradation in SiC devices [L15-025]. These sources support the existence of difficult buried interfaces and recurring reliability work. They do not establish laminography sensitivity, cabinet compatibility, or willingness to pay. The one directly relevant peer record in the pack concerns fast, high-resolution X-ray nano-tomography for advanced-packaging failure analysis [P5R2-CN-S04]. It supports the imaging job and the relevance of 3D X-ray evidence, not the startup's proposed conversion performance.

The 2030 mechanism is post-installation yield learning. JCET's primary exchange filing commits RMB 7.8 billion to a high-end advanced-packaging factory in Shanghai Lingang, with phase-one production equipment planned for completion in the second half of 2028 [P5-CNREGEN-S07]. The official 2026–2030 plan names integrated circuits among priority areas for core-technology breakthroughs [P5R2-CN-S06]. The inference is that installation is followed by process ramp, package-mix expansion, and recurring failure analysis in 2029–2034—not a committed retrofit order.

## Product boundary and retrofit qualification

The company supplies a rigid tilt-and-rotation fixture, encoded motion axes, a cabinet adapter, a calibration phantom, acquisition synchronization where permitted, reconstruction software, and a report stating validated package classes and uncertainty. V1 supports engineering-lab use; it does not claim universal compatibility, automatic production disposition, source modification, or inherited safety certification.

Qualification begins with cabinet eligibility. The source and detector must provide enough signal, field of view, magnification stability, and raw or minimally processed projections. The enclosure must have mechanical clearance for a tilted sample throughout its trajectory. The service interface must permit external motion or a documented acquisition sequence without bypassing interlocks. The stage must attach repeatably without loading the source or detector structure. If any of those conditions fail, that cabinet is outside the compatibility list.

For each eligible model, freeze the adapter drawing, motion envelope, exposure recipe, phantom procedure, reconstruction version, and repeated-registration result. “Works on brand A” is too broad because configuration may matter. The saleable object is a qualified cabinet configuration, sample envelope, and defect class—not a classifier alone.

The data asset would pair projections, reconstructions, reference truth, and process context by geometry and defect mechanism. Users need projections, slices, and artifact flags; a black-box pass/fail output creates qualification risk.

## Physics, calibration, and build path

Ordinary CT reconstructs a volume from projections distributed around the object. Flat, wide packages cannot always rotate through a complete set of angles at useful magnification because they collide with the source, detector, or enclosure. Computed laminography tilts the rotation axis relative to the beam. That geometry samples enough views to separate layers while accepting a structured missing region in spatial-frequency coverage. It is well suited in principle to planar objects, but the missing information creates anisotropic resolution and characteristic artifacts. No reconstruction algorithm can recover information that the cabinet never measured.

The load-bearing engineering problem is geometric calibration. The software needs source position, detector pose and pixel scale, rotation-axis direction, tilt angle, object pose, and their changes over the scan. Stage wobble, backlash, thermal drift, detector distortion, and sample movement all become structured reconstruction error. A calibration artifact with features distributed in three dimensions must estimate the projection matrix and provide residuals; repeated placement must then demonstrate that the estimate transfers. The frozen threshold of less than 2 micrometres repeated-registration error is a target to test, not an achieved specification.

Reconstruction should start with a transparent baseline and then add regularization. Filtered or back-projection methods can expose geometry errors quickly. Iterative algebraic reconstruction can incorporate the calibrated projection operator, while total-variation or learned priors can suppress noise and sparse-view artifacts. But a learned prior can hallucinate package-like features. Every claimed defect must therefore survive held-out geometry, seeded-blind trials, and comparison with destructive sectioning or trusted reference CT. The classifier belongs after a traceable reconstruction and should be evaluated separately from image formation.

The current evidence pack is thin on this exact physics. Seven peer-reviewed L06 papers concern plasma-tool impedance matching or electrostatic-chuck behavior rather than X-ray laminography [L06-001; L06-002; L06-003; L06-004; L06-005; L06-006; L06-007]. They illustrate rigorous modeling, control, multiphysics, and hardware qualification in semiconductor equipment, but they cannot be cited as proof of limited-angle reconstruction, calibration accuracy, or retrofit feasibility. Likewise, the four L15 packaging papers are adjacent failure-context evidence, not laminography validation [L15-001; L15-005; L15-017; L15-025]. Before product claims are published, diligence must add direct final peer-reviewed work on computed laminography geometry, limited-angle reconstruction, calibration, and uncertainty. The blinded hardware study, not adjacency, must decide the thesis.

A disciplined build proceeds in four steps. First, model two candidate cabinets from drawings and non-invasive measurements; simulate collision-free trajectories, magnification, dose, view count, and recoverable spatial frequencies. Second, build a benchtop stage and phantom and quantify axis wobble, backlash, thermal drift, and pose repeatability independent of imaging. Third, integrate with one rented cabinet using only documented interfaces, then freeze acquisition and reconstruction before inspecting blinded coupons. Fourth, port to a genuinely different cabinet brand and repeat the calibration and blind study. A one-cabinet demo is an engineering project; two-brand repeatability is the beginning of a merchant product.

## Competition and strongest bear case

The strongest bear case is that the category already exists and the retrofit layer is absorbed. The frozen red team identified three especially close threats. ZEISS NLX is an in-line 3D X-ray laminography system for advanced packaging. Fraunhofer EZRT's VOLEX offering retrofits existing industrial X-ray systems, attacking the exact installed-base premise. TRI announced a high-throughput 3D automated X-ray inspection platform in June 2026. NIST's specified ZEISS Versa XRM 730 supplies another complete-system benchmark [P5R2-CN-S03]. These claims are refresh-sensitive and must be rechecked during each design cycle, but they eliminate any claim that “3D from X-rays” or “retrofit” is empty whitespace.

The industrial X-ray supply base is substantial. Comet reported CHF 115.9 million in 2024 X-Ray Systems sales and CHF 94.6 million in Industrial X-Ray Modules sales [L05-034]. Varex reported $845 million of fiscal-2025 revenue and strong industrial activity [L05-033]. Those filings do not size advanced-packaging laminography, but they demonstrate capable source, detector, system, and service incumbents. An incumbent can bundle a stage and reconstruction mode, deny raw-data access, change firmware, restrict third-party service, or discount a new cabinet. A research institute can sell retrofit engineering. A buyer can keep using destructive sectioning for the hard cases.

The accepted catalog also documents commercially offered microfocus X-ray sources spanning 90–180 kV [P3R2-F-20-S04]. That supports component availability, but the retrofit deliberately leaves the installed source untouched and gains no proprietary advantage from source supply.

The startup has no credible 10x claim based on image novelty. The 10x case must be measured: at least tenfold lower incremental capital than a quoted complete-system alternative for a bounded defect set, or tenfold shorter time from ambiguous projection to layer-resolved disposition than send-out or serial sectioning. It must also meet sensitivity, false-positive, repeatability, and throughput thresholds. Attractive images or founder-only tuning do not qualify.

## Decisive experiment and budget integrity

The frozen experiment is a twelve-month, 100-coupon blinded study using one rented and one partner 2D cabinet. Coupons contain prespecified 10–30 micrometre voids, cracks, bridges, and layer offsets; destructive sectioning or trusted reference CT supplies the label. Freeze sample stratification, acquisition recipes, calibration, reconstruction, classifier, and analysis before unblinding.

**Technical pass:** at least 90% sensitivity across the prespecified defect set; at most 5% false positives; less than 5 minutes per package or 10 minutes per panel coupon; less than 2 micrometres repeated-registration error; and installation on two cabinet brands without source or shielding modification. Report results by defect type, depth, package geometry, and cabinet—not only an aggregate average.

**Kill:** miss any frozen sensitivity, false-positive, throughput, or repeatability threshold after the allowed calibration cycle; require source modification, shielding modification, undocumented interlock bypass, or vendor-locked service access; fail the second-brand port; or obtain no buyer-backed paid evaluation after a witnessed blind study. A research-quality reconstruction that cannot be installed, supported, and repeated is a technical success but a company failure.

The original **$92,000 design** is conditional, not a countable cheap experiment. An illustrative allocation is $18,000 for stage and adapters, $12,000 for phantoms and seeded coupons, $22,000 for cabinet time, $15,000 for reference CT or sectioning, $10,000 for compute and reconstruction work, $5,000 for safety and insurance, and $10,000 for metrology, statistics, and contingency. No signed two-cabinet access or third-party line-item quotes currently support those figures. The conservative countable planning envelope is therefore **$120,000**, and the idea receives **no sub-$100,000 portfolio credit**. Restore the $92,000 working budget only after signed cabinet access plus independent quotes for cabinet time, reference truth, fixtures, safety, and insurance make it executable.

## Bottom-up commercial arithmetic

No accepted source establishes retrofit price or installed-base size, so all commercial arithmetic is a falsifiable scenario. Assume an early qualified kit price of **$140,000**, **$25,000** of installation and applications work, and **$20,000** annual calibration/software/service per active kit. Six first-year installations would produce $990,000 of hardware-plus-integration revenue; six renewals would add $120,000 in a later full service year. Twenty-five installations would produce $4.125 million, with a 25-kit service base adding $500,000 annually. These are planning inputs, not forecasts.

Assume 45% blended gross margin on hardware and integration only after the adapter family stabilizes. Six installations then contribute about $445,500 before R&D, sales, travel, warranty, radiation-safety work, and support. At twenty-five installations, that contribution is about $1.86 million, plus service margin. If every cabinet needs a bespoke adapter, weeks of on-site tuning, or custom reconstruction, the company is an engineering consultancy and the assumed margin is invalid.

The willingness-to-pay test is equally explicit. Obtain the buyer's quoted cost and lead time for a new system, annual cost of outsourced high-resolution work, loaded cost and delay of destructive sectioning, and volume of ambiguous samples. The retrofit should pay back within eighteen months under the buyer's own queue and avoid no safety or qualification cost by assumption. If fewer than roughly ten high-value ambiguous cases per month exist, or if an incumbent upgrade is priced near the retrofit, the economic wedge may vanish despite strong imaging.

Pre-product capital of roughly **$350,000–$900,000** can support stage engineering, software, two-brand validation, and early quality systems if access is real. It does not fund a source, shielded enclosure, broad service network, or production-scale platform. The host-cabinet boundary is central to capital discipline.

## Separate US and China routes

**US route.** In 2026–2027, recruit a failure-analysis or metrology partner with a documented cabinet configuration and negotiate raw-projection, fixture, and witness rights. Build the stage and calibration stack, then run the blinded study under a written data and publication protocol. In 2028–2029, port to a second brand, pursue a paid evaluation, and map the applicable laboratory, radiation-equipment, electrical, software-security, and quality obligations with qualified counsel and test organizations. A 2030 launch should name supported configurations and defect classes rather than promise a universal converter. NIST's complete-system procurement is a benchmark and partner lead, not an assumed customer [P5R2-CN-S03].

**China route.** Use a separately governed local integration and service route for civilian packaging and substrate facilities. Manufacture the stage and calibration artifact locally where practical and independently develop the reconstruction stack used there. Screen counterparties and end uses; do not transfer controlled source or detector design; and do not assume that leaving the source untouched eliminates every technology, data, or service restriction. The two buyer tenders provide direct outreach targets for discovery of the inspection workflow, not permission to claim access or retrofit demand [P5R2-CN-S01; P5R2-CN-S02]. JCET's H2-2028 equipment milestone makes 2029 a specific time to test post-installation yield-learning appetite [P5-CNREGEN-S07].

The routes can share abstract calibration methods, test definitions, and non-sensitive reliability learning where lawful. They should maintain separate bills of material, service permissions, cybersecurity/data arrangements, and restricted-party checks. A single cross-border installation model is not the base case.

## Safety, compliance, and quality

The core safety rule is no modification of source, shielding, high voltage, or interlocks. Before motion, verify the entire tilt trajectory, cable routing, collision envelope, sample retention, emergency stop, and safe state after loss of power or communication. The adapter must not obstruct doors, shielding overlaps, detectors, source cooling, or vendor service access. Any installation needing interlock bypass is an immediate kill.

Radiation compliance attaches to the host equipment, facility, operator, and modification status. The company must obtain written cabinet-vendor and facility interpretations rather than advertise that an add-on is automatically covered by the original approval. Motion hardware also needs electrical, electromagnetic-compatibility, machinery, fire, and workplace-safety review appropriate to each route. Software changes need signed releases, versioned calibration, audit logs, access control, and rollback. Package images and process metadata may be highly confidential, so local processing and explicit retention controls should be default options.

Quality evidence must cover adapter drawing control, incoming metrology, phantom traceability, repeated calibration, software version, cybersecurity, false-positive and false-negative reporting, and field-change control. The complete result is a qualified measurement workflow, not a visually impressive reconstruction. Incumbent filings demonstrate organizations able to support installed industrial X-ray systems at scale [L05-033; L05-034]; a startup must budget applications and service accordingly.

## 2026–2034 milestones, risks, and kill dates

**2026–2027:** obtain signed access to one cabinet, complete mechanical and simulated feasibility, freeze the phantom and blind protocol, and show stable benchtop motion. Kill if raw projections are unavailable, the sample cannot move safely, or the geometry cannot be calibrated repeatably.

**2027–2028:** complete the first-cabinet blinded campaign and destructive/reference comparison. Secure the second cabinet brand before declaring success. Kill if artifacts mimic defects, the required dose or time is impractical, or results depend on manual founder tuning.

**2028–2029:** port and repeat; obtain a buyer-signed paid evaluation conditioned on frozen thresholds; document service permission, safety treatment, and a quoted bill of work. Recheck ZEISS NLX, Fraunhofer VOLEX, TRI, cabinet-vendor upgrades, and reference-system prices. Kill by **2029-12-31** if there is no paid evaluation, no two-brand compatibility, or no measured order-of-magnitude workflow advantage.

**2030 launch:** sell only named cabinet/package configurations with a calibration kit, software release, uncertainty statement, installation procedure, training, and support contract. Do not use the 2028 factory milestone as evidence of an order.

**2030–2034:** expand adapters and defect libraries only when repeatable. The durable objective is a cross-brand calibration and evidence layer that accumulates validated package-defect data. Kill the scalable-product thesis if custom engineering per installation does not fall, service burden consumes margin, cabinet vendors close interfaces, or a complete-system/retrofit incumbent matches the economics.

Principal technical risks are missing-angle artifacts, geometry drift, insufficient raw-data quality, dose/throughput tradeoffs, classifier hallucination, and sample-specific tuning. Commercial risks are access denial, warranty conflict, small ambiguous-case queues, incumbent bundling, and failure to achieve 10x economics. Regulatory risks are unintended equipment-modification status, unsafe motion, restricted end use, and mishandled customer data. These risks are correlated: a technically fragile reconstruction increases service cost and makes incumbent absorption more likely.

## Durable evidence versus refresh-sensitive claims

Durable evidence includes the existence of buried package-reliability problems [L15-001; L15-005; L15-017; L15-025], the relevance of high-resolution 3D X-ray failure analysis [P5R2-CN-S04], and the basic fact that China and US buyers procure X-ray inspection or microscopy capability [P5R2-CN-S01; P5R2-CN-S02; P5R2-CN-S03]. The principles of limited-angle information loss, calibration, blind validation, and uncertainty are durable engineering constraints even though the current accepted peer pack needs more direct laminography coverage.

Refresh-sensitive evidence includes every cabinet interface, firmware and service policy; ZEISS NLX, VOLEX, and TRI capabilities and prices; cabinet-access cost; reference-sectioning cost; the JCET construction schedule; buyer queues; and policy implementation [P5-CNREGEN-S07; P3R2-F-01-S04; P5R2-CN-S06]. Refresh in 2027, before the second-brand build, and again before the 2029 commercial decision. The broad X-ray vendor revenue context [L05-033; L05-034] should never be substituted for a count of compatible cabinets or retrofit buyers.

## Verdict

**Conditional pursue, with the frozen 55.6 score and marginal competition case intact.** The idea addresses a real and independently purchased inspection job, has a non-cosmetic stage/calibration/reconstruction boundary, and fits a 2028–2030 yield-learning window. It remains a high-kill thesis because exact retrofit demand is unproven, direct peer evidence on the proposed calibration stack is thin, VOLEX attacks the retrofit premise, and complete-system vendors can close or absorb the socket.

Spend against the conservative **$120,000** planning envelope only after signed cabinet access. Do not count the conditional $92,000 design toward the cheap-experiment quota. Advance only if two brands pass the blinded thresholds, safety and service boundaries remain intact, and a buyer signs a paid evaluation after witnessing the result. Kill promptly if the product is an attractive one-cabinet demo, a classifier without calibrated geometry, or a custom service that cannot deliver a measured 10x workflow outcome.
