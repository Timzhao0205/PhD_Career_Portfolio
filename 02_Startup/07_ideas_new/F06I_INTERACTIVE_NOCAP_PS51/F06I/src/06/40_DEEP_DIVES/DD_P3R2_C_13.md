## Thesis

The opportunity is a qualified merchant power-electronics module for laser OEMs that do not want to redesign every pump-diode current stage, interlock chain, and health monitor. The proposed product combines multi-channel GaN current switching, nanosecond-class programmable edges, deterministic safety interlocks, waveform capture, and diode-health telemetry. A firmware layer exposes pump-modulation hooks that can be tested against transverse-mode-instability (TMI) behavior in high-power fiber amplifiers. The initial company is therefore a subsystem supplier, not a laser maker, weapon-system integrator, diode-fabrication company, or generic GaN component vendor.

There is a real technical substrate. Peer-reviewed work compares modulation shapes in laser-diode drivers [L12-025], demonstrates digitally adjustable picosecond-resolution drive [L12-026], and reports a GaN-HEMT pulsed driver above 100 W for 10–100 ns pulses [L12-027]. Separate research describes TMI as a power-scaling limit and models its dependence on excitation, gain, pump depletion, and thermal dynamics [L12-016; L12-017; L12-018]. High-power laser architectures are also advancing: coherent-combination work demonstrates controllable beam shaping [L12-001], while another experiment reports 403 W at 260 fs with precise dispersion management [L12-002]. Those sources establish that fast drive electronics, laser stability, and control remain active engineering domains.

They do **not** establish a merchant socket. The closest competitors are the in-house electronics teams of vertically integrated laser OEMs. They regard diode life, safety, modulation, and system stability as core product knowledge. The entire venture turns on whether a qualified external module can reduce their engineering and qualification burden without surrendering control of laser performance. That is why the first decisive experiment is a $72,000 make-versus-buy and paid-evaluation gate, not the more photogenic $180,000 eight-channel build.

## Buyer, job, and demand evidence

The plausible US discovery counterparties are nLIGHT, Coherent, smaller industrial-laser OEMs, and US directed-energy integrators that source laser subsystems. Their painful job is specific: deliver repeatable pump current across channels; protect expensive diode stacks during faults and degraded cooling; coordinate start, stop, and emergency interlocks; diagnose threshold and droop drift before field failure; and requalify the electronics when diode, thermal, or optical configurations change. A merchant supplier wins only if an OEM assigns that complete electrical boundary to it.

The host-system demand is substantial. nLIGHT reported 2025 revenue of $261.3 million, including $179.2 million in its Laser Products segment and $175.3 million from aerospace and defense, with a $161.6 million funded backlog and additional unfunded government-contract value [L12-033]. A separate industry report ties that growth to named programs and reports the same $175 million aerospace-and-defense scale [P3R2-C-13-S04]. The 2026 Joint Laser Weapon System award provides a nearer timing marker: the government program reportedly began with $86 million across two awardees and an $847 million total ceiling [L12-031], while nLIGHT's portion was reported as $44 million initially with a ceiling up to $627 million [L12-032; P3R2-C-13-S03]. Demonstrations were described as possible as early as 2028 [P3R2-C-13-S03]. Coherent, meanwhile, identifies industrial lasers, semiconductor equipment, precision manufacturing, and instrumentation as growth domains [L12-036].

These records prove active laser-system budgets, not an order for an outside driver. Indeed, nLIGHT's integration from chip toward system level is evidence against outsourcing. The correct reading of G1 is therefore marginal: funded systems create a buyer ecosystem, but only a paid module evaluation would establish the startup's job.

The China demand story is also category-level. Han's Laser reported 2025 revenue of RMB 18.759 billion, but high-power laser-equipment revenue fell 6.6% even as high-power cutting-machine unit sales rose 30.47% to 6,800 [L12-037]. That combination supports the price-pressure premise. It can motivate outsourcing of a well-bounded non-core module, but it can just as easily favor already-amortized internal designs and compress supplier margins. An official Dongguan 2020–2035 industrial plan explicitly prioritizes high-power fiber, semiconductor, and ultrafast lasers, upstream components, pump-source packaging, standardization, and industrialization [P5-G7CNREQ-S04]. That is the named 2035 category trigger supporting G7, although it does not name a driver procurement.

Raycus's 2025 report—RMB 3.467 billion in revenue, 186,000 laser units, and 8,561 units above 10 kW—shows the scale and intensity of domestic competition [L12-038]. Its Q1 2026 results supply an independent current-market timing signal [P3R2-C-13-S05]. **Raycus is excluded from the reachable-buyer boundary.** The evidence pack records its addition to the US Entity List and a presumption-of-denial posture for covered transfers [P3R2-C-13-S01; P3R2-C-13-S02]. Its financials may inform market structure and incumbent capability; they cannot be used to imply a lawful prospective customer. A China beachhead must instead begin with a freshly screened civilian industrial OEM, such as Han's Laser only after counsel and ownership/end-use review.

## Product boundary and controlled build

The v1 is an **eight-channel driver and telemetry module**, not a complete laser power supply. Each channel controls a defined diode-string interface; several channels operate in parallel to reach a nominal 1 kA aggregate envelope. The module includes gate drive, current sensing, programmable edge and waveform control, current-sharing supervision, thermal inputs, hardware overcurrent protection, independent shutdown, watchdogs, event capture, and a deterministic host interface. Diode-health telemetry tracks changes in threshold, droop, channel balance, and thermal response. The firmware exposes bounded modulation commands and synchronized logs so the OEM can evaluate stability without surrendering the outer laser-control loop.

The company should not build pump diodes, fiber, combiners, optical isolators, beam directors, or complete directed-energy systems. It should not claim ownership of the TMI solution. TMI depends on optical mode content, heating, gain saturation, pump depletion, and fiber design [L12-016; L12-017]. The module merely offers precise, repeatable electrical actuation and measurement that lets an OEM test whether pump shaping helps at its operating point. Likewise, the pulsed-driver literature clears a buildability gate [L12-026; L12-027], but a gain-switched sub-nanosecond driver and a high-average-power pump bank are different duty regimes. Bridging them demands new thermal, lifetime, current-sharing, and electromagnetic-interference evidence.

The controlled build path is staged. First, develop one isolated channel against an electronic diode emulator, measuring edge repeatability, overshoot, ringing, protection latency, current-sense accuracy, and failure containment. Second, operate four and then eight channels into representative electrical loads to expose common-source inductance, ground-bounce, timing skew, and current-sharing failure. Third, connect to a university-class fiber-amplifier test stand only after independent electrical and optical safety review. The test must compare a fixed-current baseline with predeclared modulation profiles across pump power, thermal condition, and mode-excitation states. It must report optical output, beam-quality or stability metrics, diode electrical stress, false trips, and thermal margin—not merely an attractive current waveform.

The physics risks are ordinary but unforgiving. Fast GaN edges amplify parasitic inductance and capacitive coupling. Parallel channels can current-hog. A gate-driver upset can overdrive an expensive diode stack before software responds. Sensor drift can turn health telemetry into false reassurance. Protection therefore needs a hardware path independent of firmware, energy-limited abnormal-state tests, safe discharge, galvanic partitioning where required, and fault injection covering loss of auxiliary power, stuck commands, open sensors, shorted devices, cooling loss, and interlock disagreement. Production credibility will depend more on accumulated stress and failure data than on a single peak-current demonstration.

## Incumbents, substitutes, and strongest bear case

The named incumbents are nLIGHT, Coherent, Han's Laser, and Raycus—not because all sell an identical merchant board, but because their internal power-electronics and controls groups perform the exact make function the startup wants them to buy. nLIGHT's scale and backlog support demand while increasing its capacity to keep driver IP inside [L12-033]. Coherent has the breadth to bundle sources, electronics, and applications knowledge [L12-036]. Han's operates at manufacturing scale under price pressure [L12-037]. Raycus, although excluded as a buyer, demonstrates the volume and vertical depth of a domestic competitor [L12-038]. Analog-semiconductor reference designs, bespoke contract manufacturers, and an OEM's last-generation board are cheaper substitutes.

The strongest bear case is that the thesis splices together three adjacent but non-equivalent facts. Fast laser-diode drivers exist [L12-025; L12-026; L12-027]. TMI constrains power scaling [L12-016; L12-017; L12-018]. Large laser-system programs are funded [L12-031; L12-032]. None proves that nanosecond pump modulation improves a production high-power fiber laser enough for an integrated OEM to requalify around a startup module. The OEM can instead change fiber geometry, mode filtering, thermal management, pump configuration, or its own control algorithm. Even if the module works, a paid design can be absorbed after one generation or forced into a low-margin build-to-print contract.

The US–China scale-economy story is weaker still. A clean separation requires different personnel access, repositories, firmware, component sourcing, technical-data flows, governance, and counterparty screening. A two-entity structure does not cleanse controlled US technology. US defense customers may reject shared ownership or engineering links, while the China entity may need a domestic bill of materials and independent development. The analysis therefore assumes **no cross-market volume economy** until counsel and buyers establish otherwise.

## The 10x requirement

Nanosecond edges are an engineering attribute, not a 10x customer outcome. The frozen 10x score remains low. A tenfold claim should earn credit only if the OEM predeclares one of three results and the module demonstrates it against the OEM's board: at least 10x less engineering time to retarget a diode/pump configuration; at least 10x earlier detection of a defined degradation precursor at the same false-alarm rate; or at least 10x lower stability variance under a prescribed disturbance without increasing diode electrical stress. A fourth possibility is a 10x reduction in qualification test setup through standardized telemetry and replay, but only buyer time sheets can support it.

The evidence pack cannot yet support any of those outcomes. L12-025 connects modulation shape to diode performance, and L12-026 demonstrates very fine timing control, but neither establishes the proposed OEM-valued delta. The durable wedge would be the combination of qualified hardware, fault and aging data, and firmware validated across multiple diode configurations. If the value reduces to faster edges or a lower bill of materials, the large OEMs and semiconductor vendors can match it.

## Cheapest decisive merchant-socket experiment

The frozen first experiment is a **$72,000 cross-market make-versus-buy and paid-evaluation gate** over roughly four months. No current access, signed evaluation, or purchase order is assumed.

Budget $12,000 for two reference interface-control documents and a credible module bill of materials; $9,000 for an electrical/thermal risk model and representative waveforms; $8,000 for a safety, reliability, and qualification plan; $14,000 for structured US OEM discovery and applications engineering; $14,000 for screened China civilian-OEM discovery, translation, and local technical support; $11,000 for export-control, ownership, end-use, and contracting counsel; and $4,000 for an independent review of the pass/fail record. The team should approach at least six US commercial or defense-adjacent OEM/integrator organizations and six screened China civilian industrial-laser OEMs through counsel-approved channels.

Every discussion starts from the same questions: Which current-stage functions remain in-house? Which interface would the buyer permit a merchant module to own? What reliability, EMI, safety, lifetime, and service evidence would qualify it? Would the buyer provide representative loads and waveforms? Will it pay at least $25,000 for a bounded evaluation with named deliverables? Verbal interest, a meeting, an NDA, free lab access, or a generic letter earns no pass credit.

**Pass:** two paid evaluation agreements—one US and one screened China civilian counterparty—each define the electrical boundary, supplied test conditions, decision owner, evaluation criteria, and a 2028–2029 design-in path. Counsel must also provide a written architecture showing that the US and China work can proceed without prohibited technology or data flow.

**Kill or fold:** fewer than two paid evaluations by the end of 2028; both counterparties retain the driver as core in-house IP; the allowed interface shrinks to a commodity board; or the separation conditions make the US opportunity incompatible with China participation. On failure, stop the standalone company and treat the power stage as an internal product line for a broader laser-control business. On pass, release the separate approximately $180,000 eight-channel technical build. The commercial gate does not prove the physics; it proves that the physics would occupy a merchant socket.

## Bottom-up commercial arithmetic

No source in the pack provides a merchant-driver price, module count per laser, or outsourcing rate. The only honest arithmetic is an explicit scenario. Assume an early qualified eight-channel module has a $30,000 average selling price, four modules are used per qualifying high-value laser system, and the company wins three OEM programs shipping 40 systems per year. That yields 480 modules and **$14.4 million annual revenue**. At an illustrative 45% gross margin, gross profit is $6.48 million before applications engineering, warranty reserves, compliance, and support.

An expansion case of six programs, 80 systems per year, and four modules per system yields 1,920 modules and **$57.6 million annual revenue**. A downside case of two programs, 20 systems per year, two modules per system, and a price compressed to $20,000 yields only **$1.6 million**. The four input variables—qualified programs, systems per program, modules per system, and price—therefore dominate the result. Host-market revenue cannot replace them.

For a rough break-even test, assume a 24-person engineering, applications, quality, and operations team costs $6 million fully loaded, with another $3 million for facilities, test access, counsel, insurance, and qualification. At a 45% gross margin, the company needs $20 million of annual revenue, or about **667 modules at $30,000 each**, before growth investment. That is about 167 four-module systems. If discovery shows one module per system, sub-$15,000 pricing, or only a handful of annual systems, the standalone economics fail even with excellent technology. The frozen $5–12 million v1 capital range is plausible only with staged builds and customer-funded evaluation; clean-room duplication can push it higher.

## Separate US and China routes

**US route.** In 2026–2027, complete the $72,000 socket test, one-channel emulator work, and a written technology-control plan. Target a commercial industrial OEM first, where waveforms and telemetry can be tested without defense-program data. In 2027–2028, a paid evaluation should define the eight-channel build and reliability matrix. Any directed-energy work stays in a US-controlled line with need-to-know repositories, screened personnel, segregated firmware, traceable components, and buyer-approved cybersecurity and data handling. The 2028 JLWS demonstration marker means waiting until 2030 to seek the first architecture discussion is too late [P3R2-C-13-S03]. A 2030 launch is credible only if the interface and evaluation begin pre-company.

**China route.** Establish a separately governed, independently developed, domestic-origin commercial product line for civilian industrial lasers. Begin with a screened OEM or qualified local channel; Han's is only a discovery example and requires fresh official-list, ownership, and end-use screening before contact. The Dongguan plan supports a 2035 pump-component industrialization window [P5-G7CNREQ-S04], while Han's financials show current volume and price pressure [L12-037]. Neither is a commitment. Raycus remains outside the buyer set regardless of its market scale [P3R2-C-13-S01; P3R2-C-13-S02]. No US defense design data, firmware, or controlled component should cross into the China route.

The routes may share public physics, independently developed test abstractions, and generic safety lessons only when counsel approves. They should not share a common codebase or bill of materials by default. If a US buyer requires ownership and engineering separation that cannot coexist with the China entity, the company must choose the US route and drop China rather than reinterpret the restriction.

## Safety, compliance, and qualification

The module combines high current, fast voltage transitions, stored energy, hot surfaces, and a host Class-4-laser environment. The electrical enclosure needs touch-safe service states, forced discharge, creepage and clearance appropriate to the final voltage and contamination class, fire-resistant materials, interlocked connectors, and energy-limited fault containment. Development testing requires remote operation, physical barriers, an external emergency disconnect, diode-stack or load protection independent of the prototype, and separate electrical and laser safety officers.

Functional-safety work should begin with a hazard analysis and failure-mode-and-effects analysis. Emergency shutdown, overcurrent, overtemperature, cooling-loss, enclosure, and optical-system interlocks need hardwired behavior that remains safe under processor crash, corrupt firmware, sensor open/short, lost communications, and auxiliary-power failure. Configuration control must make firmware provenance and field updates auditable. EMI qualification is core product performance: excessive common-mode current can corrupt current sensing, upset neighboring controls, or couple into optical instrumentation.

Compliance must be determined per buyer and end use, not asserted from a generic certificate. The company should map applicable electrical-equipment, laser-system, electromagnetic-compatibility, machinery, cybersecurity, and functional-safety standard families with the OEM, then allocate responsibility in the interface contract. US defense work adds export classification, controlled-technical-data handling, supply-chain representations, and potentially program-specific cybersecurity. China work adds independent origin, local product classification, counterparty, ownership, and end-use screening. Counsel clearance is a gate, not an after-the-fact memo.

Measure customer-valued laser stability, diode stress, thermal behavior, EMI, and reliability. Do not count current edge speed alone.
- **2029:** complete environmental and endurance campaigns, close supply-chain and firmware controls, and require at least one paid design-in with a 2030 production decision in each continuing route. If the China route cannot be lawfully and operationally separated, drop it. If neither buyer assigns the merchant boundary, fold the technology and stop the company.

## Roadmap: 2030–2034

In **2030**, release one tightly qualified commercial SKU rather than a broad family. Applications engineering should focus on two diode configurations and a controlled firmware surface. In **2031**, convert evaluation data into repeatable production tests, serialized telemetry calibration, field-return analysis, and a second-source plan for critical GaN, sensing, and isolation components. In **2032**, add a higher-current or higher-channel-count variant only if it reuses the qualification corpus and the original SKU has positive contribution margin.

In **2033**, the durable expansion is a common health and qualification data layer across multiple laser platforms, not entry into complete laser systems. The US defense-adjacent line may scale with funded production options, but the award ceilings are not guaranteed revenue [L12-031; L12-032]. By **2034**, kill the standalone venture if it has fewer than three production programs, cannot sustain 40% gross margin after support and warranty, or remains dependent on one buyer whose in-house team can absorb the design.

## Risks and explicit kill conditions

1. **No merchant socket:** no two paid evaluations by end-2028. Kill or fold.
2. **Regime mismatch:** pulsed-driver performance fails to translate to high-average-power pump operation. Kill the affected SKU if current sharing, thermal margin, or lifetime misses the buyer baseline.
3. **No optical outcome:** pump modulation does not improve a predeclared stability, lifetime, or qualification metric. Remove TMI claims; kill if telemetry alone cannot support the economics.
4. **Incumbent absorption:** OEMs accept only build-to-print work or demand ownership of firmware and data at commodity margins. Do not finance a standalone company.
5. **Compliance incompatibility:** the US route cannot coexist with China ownership, personnel, data, or sourcing. Choose one route; do not manufacture a dual-market score.
6. **China access failure:** no screened civilian counterparty, or the domestic-origin product cannot be developed without prohibited transfers. Drop China. Raycus cannot repair this failure.
7. **Qualification capital:** required reliability, EMI, safety, and program-specific testing push capital beyond the staged $5–12 million plan without customer funding. Stop before full production tooling.
8. **Revenue concentration:** fewer than three programs or one customer above 50% of revenue by 2034. Seek a strategic sale or wind down rather than accept captive-supplier economics.

## Durable versus refresh-sensitive evidence

**Durable:** GaN can produce fast, controllable laser-diode drive [L12-026; L12-027]; modulation waveform affects diode performance [L12-025]; TMI is a real high-power-fiber-laser limit with well-developed physical models [L12-016; L12-017; L12-018]; laser OEMs have strong incentives to retain control of safety, lifetime, and stability; and qualified reliability data can compound across product generations.

**Refresh-sensitive:** nLIGHT revenue, backlog, JLWS award values, production options, and demonstration timing [L12-033; L12-031; P3R2-C-13-S03]; Coherent's growth priorities [L12-036]; Han's and Raycus financial and unit-volume conditions [L12-037; L12-038; P3R2-C-13-S05]; the execution of Dongguan's 2035 plan [P5-G7CNREQ-S04]; official restricted-party status and licensing policy; acceptable counterparties; component origin; OEM make-versus-buy policy; and whether the 2028–2029 design-in window remains open. These items require refresh before every paid engagement and again before a 2030 launch.

## Verdict

**Proceed only as a paid-socket option; do not yet fund the standalone hardware company.** The 54.2 score and marginal G1, G4, G6, and G7 verdicts are appropriate. The concept is technically buildable, unusually aligned with fast-power and instrumentation skills, and exposed to real laser-system demand. Its central commercial premise—integrated OEMs buying a merchant driver—remains unproven, its 10x outcome is undefined, and the two-market structure adds cost rather than assured leverage.

Spend $72,000 to force the boundary decision. If one US and one screened China civilian buyer pay for evaluations under a lawful separation architecture, release the eight-channel build and measure an OEM-valued outcome. If not, fold the driver and telemetry work into a broader laser-control product and stop. Raycus remains excluded from the buyer boundary under every branch.
