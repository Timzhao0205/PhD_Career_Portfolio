# D07 — P3R2-D-01: Merchant quench detection and protection for HTS magnets

## 1. Thesis

The high-temperature-superconductor magnet industry now sells magnets as
merchant products, but nobody sells the subsystem that keeps them alive.
FACT: Commonwealth Fusion Systems has installed TF1 — the first of
eighteen 24-tonne, 20 T, >30,000 A HTS magnets — in the SPARC Tokamak
Hall, targets first plasma in 2026 and net energy in 2027, with all
eighteen magnets on site by end of summer 2026 [P3R2-D-01-S01, R10-031].
FACT: CFS sells magnets to third parties — the University of Wisconsin
WHAM experiment, a technology license to Type One, and an April 2026
strategic partnership making CFS the magnet designer-manufacturer for
Realta Fusion's mirror plants, described as its largest such deal and
potentially multi-billion-dollar [L03-035, R10-035]. FACT: quench in
REBCO magnets is physically harder to detect than in the low-temperature
superconductors that trained the industry — normal zones propagate
slowly, so the classic voltage-tap signal arrives late — and the
peer-reviewed protection literature says so directly [L03-004], while
strain, delamination and screening-current stresses give REBCO coils
failure modes the LTS era never qualified [L03-018, L03-021].
INFERENCE: multi-megajoule stored energy plus slow intrinsic detection
plus a growing population of magnets owned by parties who did not build
them equals a safety-critical market for independent detection and
protection. HYPOTHESIS: a merchant quench-detection-and-protection
subsystem — co-wound distributed fiber-optic sensing fused with RF and
voltage channels in deterministic electronics, plus qualified
sub-millisecond energy extraction, delivered with a certification-grade
qualification dossier — becomes the flight recorder and ejection seat
of the HTS magnet era.

## 2. Customer job and named buyers

The customer job is to detect a quench before thermal runaway, dump
stored energy before the coil damages itself, and prove both
capabilities to whoever bears the asset risk. The buyer population now
includes magnet owners who are not magnet builders — the structural
fact the thesis stands on. Named buyers: Realta Fusion and
WHAM-class academic owners operating purchased CFS magnets [L03-035,
R10-035]; the eight DOE Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program
companies (CFS and Tokamak Energy named), a cohort that has raised over
$350M of private capital against $46M of federal obligations [L03-032];
Tokamak Energy/TE Magnetics, explicitly commercializing HTS magnets for
fusion, science, mobility and security markets on the back of a $125M
raise [L03-033, L03-044]; US ITER/ORNL, whose completed central
solenoid program demonstrates the national-lab procurement channel for
magnet-adjacent subsystems [L03-030]; and, on the research frontier,
ASIPP-class institutes running 35.1 T fully superconducting magnets
[L03-029] — in China's case strictly as a license-out footnote, not a
market. Side-channel buyers: the KEPCO/LS superconducting cable and
datacenter-grid programs, whose fault-current-limiter and cable
protection needs share the sensing physics [L03-042, L03-043].

## 3. Product boundary and architecture

The product is the protection layer, sold as a subsystem: (a) co-wound
distributed fiber-optic sensing (Rayleigh-backscatter interrogation
giving centimeter-scale, kHz-rate strain/temperature profiles along
kilometers of conductor) fused with RF pickup and voltage taps; (b) a
deterministic detection engine — FPGA-class sensor fusion with
qualified false-trigger statistics; (c) energy-extraction electronics
executing sub-millisecond dump decisions at coil scale, in the design
lineage of the published fast-protection systems [L03-008]; and (d)
the qualification dossier — MQE/NZPV test data, detection-latency
distributions, dump-circuit certification — that converts instruments
into a safety case. Detection methods and protection modeling from the
literature define the baseline the product must beat [L03-006, L03-005,
L03-007]. Explicitly outside the boundary: magnet design, winding,
cryogenics, and tape QC (the acceptance-metrology socket is its own
idea in this portfolio, P3R2-D-02). The moat is the qualification
dataset accumulated across owners and magnet classes — an asset no
single builder's in-house team accumulates, because each sees only its
own coils.

## 4. Feasibility

Every element is demonstrated component technology, and the conductor
class itself is settled: the review literature establishes REBCO coated
conductors as the enabling technology for next-generation tokamak
magnets [L03-001], which is why the protection problem is worth solving
as a product rather than a per-project engineering task. Distributed
fiber interrogation is commercial; RF quench pickup and voltage-tap
methods are published [L03-006, L03-007]; energy-extraction circuits at
magnet scale are established engineering [L03-008]; REBCO conductor
behavior — critical-current surfaces at ultra-high field [L03-020],
strain degradation [L03-021], delamination and screening-current
mechanics [L03-018] — is peer-reviewed and defines what the sensors
must see. The integration leap is fusion-and-qualification: proving
that fiber-plus-RF-plus-voltage, fused deterministically, gives ≥100 ms
of warning before thermal runaway with <1% false triggers on real
driven quenches. Three engineering sub-problems deserve honest
treatment. First, sensor survivability: co-wound fiber must survive
winding tension, epoxy impregnation, thermal cycling to 20 K, and
magnet-lifetime radiation — a materials-and-process qualification
task the 2027 campaign starts. Second, fusion statistics: false dumps
are nearly as costly as missed quenches (a dump cycles the magnet and
halts the machine), so the detection engine's value is its
false-trigger rate under operational noise — a signal-processing
problem in the founder's core stack. Third, dump-circuit
qualification at scale: laboratory extraction circuits [L03-008] must
become certified, redundant, maintainable safety hardware — the
CERN-lineage LTS protection world (CLIQ and successors) is prior art
tuned to different physics [L03-004], leaving the HTS-specific
qualification genuinely open.

## 5. Measurable 10x edge

Voltage taps on REBCO structurally fail at the task: slow normal-zone
propagation means millivolt signals emerge only when runaway is already
local and hot [L03-004]. Centimeter-resolved, kHz-rate distributed
sensing along the entire conductor — versus point measurements at
terminals — is an orders-of-magnitude jump in observability, and the
fusion of modalities converts it into warning time: the decisive
experiment's ≥100 ms target against the effectively-zero usable warning
of tap-only schemes on thick REBCO windings. The edge is benchable on
driven-quench campaigns and is precisely what a qualification dossier
sells.

## 6. Demand

Durable demand evidence: the merchant magnet market itself — CFS
selling to Realta and WHAM and licensing Type One [L03-035, R10-035];
the DOE Milestone cohort and its leverage ratio [L03-032]; Tokamak
Energy's dedicated magnet-commercialization vehicle and Furukawa wire
supply chain [L03-033, L03-044]; US ITER's completed central solenoid
as the lab-channel proof [L03-030]. Refresh-sensitive evidence: SPARC's
2026-2027 milestones [P3R2-D-01-S01, R10-031] — the single most
important schedule in the demand case — and the April 2026 Realta
partnership [R10-035]. Honest statement, carried from P4: protection-
specific RFPs do not exist yet; this is TRL-3-appropriate,
ecosystem-level demand, and the venture's 2028 milestone exists
precisely to convert it into product demand.

Bottom-up arithmetic (every input labeled). FACT: SPARC alone contains
18 magnets at 20 T and >30,000 A [P3R2-D-01-S01]; ARC-class plants are
targeted for the early 2030s [P3R2-D-01-S01]; Realta's program spans
demonstration and commercial mirror plants [R10-035]. ASSUMPTION:
protection content (sensing, detection engine, dump electronics,
qualification) is worth $0.5-2M per large fusion system and
$50-300k per research magnet — per-system pricing is unsourced, a
red-team-flagged gap. INFERENCE: the named 2030-2034 build pipeline
(SPARC-successors, Realta plants, milestone-cohort pilots, lab
magnets) supports a $20-100M cumulative protection opportunity, with
NMR, accelerator and cable protection [L03-042, L03-043] as
extensions. The load-bearing conclusion: one fusion design win plus
the research-magnet base funds the company; the arithmetic needs no
heroic fusion outcome — only that funded builders keep building coils.

## 7. Competition

No merchant integrated HTS detection-plus-dump vendor exists — verified
at P4 and re-verified at refresh. The real competition: in-house
protection teams at CFS and TE Magnetics; the CERN CLIQ lineage,
LTS-tuned prior art [L03-004]; component instrumentation vendors
selling interrogators and electronics without qualification or
integration; and — the deepest threat — the no-insulation winding
approach, which trades away fast controllability for intrinsic
self-protection and could shrink the detection problem at pilot scale.
The red team's strongest counterargument deserves its full weight:
magnet builders are the parties best equipped to build protection, and
they may no more outsource it than winding itself.

### 7a. Competitive scenarios and response plan

Scenario A (most likely): builders keep in-house protection for their
own machines while merchant magnet owners (Realta/WHAM-class) need
independent protection and acceptance evidence for purchased coils.
Response: standard plan — the beachhead is the owner side, where the
SEL analogy actually holds because the protector is not the vendor
being checked. Scenario B: CFS publishes or productizes its internal
protection stack, setting a de facto standard. Response: build to
interoperate — offer qualification services and independent
verification of CFS-standard installations, and hold the
research-magnet and non-fusion tiers (NMR, accelerators [L03-029],
cables [L03-043]) where CFS will not go. Scenario C: no-insulation
windings dominate pilot-scale designs. Response: NI coils still
quench, still store megajoules, and still need monitoring — the
product pivots toward thermal-runaway observability and asset-health
monitoring for NI systems, keeping the sensing layer while the dump
electronics tier shrinks; the fiber dataset transfers intact.
Scenario D: the DOE appropriations gap ($415M authorized vs ~$98M
appropriated through FY2027, tracked annually) starves the cohort.
Response: CFS/TE private capital and the lab channel [L03-030] carry
the near-term market; the venture's cost base stays sized to the
research-magnet tier until fusion procurement resumes.

## 8. Decisive 2026-2029 experiment

2027 university-lab campaign: co-wind distributed fiber and RF pickup
into a 1-2 T REBCO pancake coil; run driven-quench campaigns across
operating points; fuse fiber/RF/voltage channels in a deterministic
detection engine. Budget: $250,000. Thresholds: ≥100 ms warning before
thermal runaway across the driven-quench matrix; <1% false-trigger
rate under operational noise; fiber surviving winding, impregnation
and thermal cycling with calibrated response [L03-021, L03-018]. KILL
RULE: if fused sensing cannot deliver 100 ms warning on a real REBCO
coil, or false triggers cannot be driven below 1%, the merchant
protection thesis fails and the work folds to sensing-component
licensing. Follow-on (red-team dated repair): one magnet-builder
co-test agreement — WHAM-class academic magnet acceptable — signed by
2028, converting ecosystem demand into product demand; 2029: dump-
electronics prototype qualified against the published fast-protection
baseline [L03-008].

## 9. 2030-2034 timing

SPARC results in 2026-2027 [P3R2-D-01-S01] convert, on CFS's own
schedule, into ARC-class and Realta pilot-plant magnet builds in the
early 2030s [R10-035] — which is when protection procurement becomes
real line items. Qualification data accumulated 2027-2029 is the moat
at exactly that moment: a bidder with three years of driven-quench
statistics on instrumented REBCO coils is untouchable by a fresh
entrant. Launch 2030; adoption 2030-2034: protection design-ins on
two-plus pilot-plant programs, the research-magnet installed base, and
first cable-system deployments through the KEPCO/LS channel [L03-043].
The fusion-schedule risk is real and is hedged by the non-fusion tiers
— NMR, accelerator, and 35 T-class research magnets [L03-029] quench
regardless of fusion's fortunes. The timing logic also runs in
reverse: if SPARC's 2026-2027 results disappoint, the installed
research-magnet base still grows on national-lab and university
budgets, and a protection vendor sized to that tier survives to see
the next fusion cycle — the launch date flexes, the physics demand
does not.

## 10. Independent US and China paths

US-only by adjudication: the buyer cohort, labs, and capital are
American [L03-032, L03-030, L03-035]; the venture is a US entity.
China appears solely as a strictly-gated licensing footnote —
ASIPP/CFETR-class institutes [L03-029] could license sensing
technology under counsel-reviewed terms, but no CN beachhead, revenue,
or entity is claimed or planned; stage-20 screening found the
instrumentation and power-electronics classes uncontrolled, and the
gate is counsel review, not license-in-hand. The portfolio's US/China
balance is carried by other ideas; this one's contribution is honesty.

## 11. Side markets

South Korea: the KEPCO 23 kV commercial HTS cable lineage and the
July 2025 LS Cable/LS Electric/KEPCO datacenter superconducting-grid
MOU create a protection-and-monitoring channel for cable systems and
fault-current limiters [L03-042, L03-043]. Japan: the Furukawa/

partnership natural. Both optional and additive to the US leg.

## 12. Regulation, safety, export controls

No dedicated certification regime yet governs HTS magnet protection —
the qualification dossier is therefore both product and future
standard-input, the same dynamic as the portfolio's other
method-authority plays. Fusion pilot plants will be licensed
facilities; protection evidence of exactly this kind will be part of
their safety cases. Export: instrumentation and dump-electronics
classes are not named on control lists (stage-20 screening); the CN
licensing footnote is counsel-gated; no other trigger identified.

## 13. Capital and business model

v1 capital $3-8M: two qualification campaigns, detection-engine
productization, and dump-electronics certification. Business model:
protection subsystems at safety-critical margins, recurring
calibration and health-monitoring services on the installed base, and
qualification/verification services (scenario B's hedge). The $250k
experiment is cheap relative to the claim it buys; the co-test
agreement converts credibility into a named customer before launch
capital is raised.

## 14. Founder skill transfer

Direct fit: distributed-sensor interrogation, deterministic FPGA
fusion, low-noise analog channels, dump-circuit power electronics,
and statistical qualification are the founder's instrumentation core.
Cryogenic magnet practice is the gap — closed through the
university-lab campaign structure and the co-test partner rather than
by pretending expertise.

## 15. Failure modes

(1) NI windings suffice at pilot scale — scenario C; the sensing
layer survives, the dump tier shrinks. (2) CFS in-house standard —
scenario B. (3) Appropriations starvation — scenario D. (4) Demand
never converts from ecosystem to product — the 2028 co-test milestone
is the tripwire; failing it triggers portfolio-level reassessment
before launch capital. (5) Fiber survivability in real windings —
caught at $250k by the kill rule.

## 15a. Residual risk assessment

Red-team decision: survive, medium residual risk, medium confidence —
the most conditional verdict in the top ten, and this report keeps
that character visible rather than smoothing it. What earns the slot
despite it: the physics gap is real and peer-reviewed [L03-004], the
merchant-owner class is new and growing [R10-035], the experiment is
cheap, and every failure mode has a dated tripwire (SPARC results
2026-2027, co-test by 2028, appropriations annually). The idea is a
call option on the HTS build-out, priced at $250k with a 2028
exercise decision — and options on the safety-critical layer of a new
infrastructure class have historically been cheap ones worth holding.
