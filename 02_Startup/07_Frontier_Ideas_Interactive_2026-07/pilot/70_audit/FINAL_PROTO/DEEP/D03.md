# D03 — P3R2-C-05: Standards-grade thermal emulation and conformance metrology for liquid cooling

## 1. Thesis

Liquid cooling became mandatory infrastructure before anyone built its
measurement layer. FACT: NVIDIA's GB200 NVL72 rack is liquid-cooled by
design necessity, with third-party technical estimates above 120 kW of rack
heat [L14-044], and the peer-reviewed roadmap puts AI accelerator TDP on a
3,000-5,000 W trajectory with chip heat flux moving toward 800-1,000 W/cm²
[L14-010]. FACT: Google published its Project Deschutes 2 MW CDU
specification — 3 °C approach-temperature difference, ~80 PSI, fully
redundant pump-circuit feeds, 0.2-micron side-stream filtration — and
eight named vendors (Boyd, CoolerMaster, Delta, Envicool, Nidec, nVent,
Stulz, Vertiv) now build to it [L14-043, P3R2-C-05-S02]. FACT: OCP's
cooling standardization remains partial — draft coolant guidelines, a
quick-disconnect spec still in revision, and no complete conformance test
method with reference hardware [R10-023]. FACT: China's GB 40879 energy
efficiency standard is being tightened toward PUE ≤1.25 for new large
datacenters, and the T/CIEP 0263-2025 group standard now specifies
liquid-cooling system design, inspection and acceptance across 40-plus
drafting organizations [L14-035, L14-039]. INFERENCE: when a buyer-authored
specification governs billions of dollars of hardware and no independent
method exists to verify conformance, a qualification vacuum exists on both
sides of the Pacific. HYPOTHESIS: a merchant metrology vendor — selling
programmable multi-kilowatt thermal test vehicles, rack-scale load
emulators, traceable calorimetry, and a round-robin conformance protocol —
becomes the method authority of the liquid-cooling era, the position JEDEC
benches occupy in semiconductors.

## 2. Customer job and named buyers

The customer job is proof: demonstrate, with traceable numbers, that a cold
plate, CDU, or immersion system performs to specification before a
warranty, a PUE commitment, or an acceptance test puts money on the line.
Named buyers, vendor side: the eight Deschutes-spec CDU builders
[P3R2-C-05-S02], of which Nidec already exhibits a compliant 2 MW-class
prototype with IEEE-519 low-harmonic VFD [P3R2-C-05-S01]; the
qualification-hungry direct-to-chip class typified by JetCool (acquired by
Flex, with CDUs spanning 300 kW racks to 2 MW rows) [L14-045] and ZutaCore
(a $100M+ Series C in June 2026 from Mitsubishi Electric, Carrier and
Samsung investors, 75+ deployments to defend) [L14-046]. Named buyers,
buyer side: hyperscaler procurement organizations enforcing the very specs
they authored [L14-043], and Vertiv-scale integrators whose $15.0 billion
backlog and ~252% Q4-2025 order growth ride on cooling performance
commitments [L14-048]. Named buyers, institutional side: national
verification facilities of the class NTT opened in 2025 (780 kW of mock
load, air and liquid) [L14-050] and Taiwan's ITRI two-phase cold-plate
program [L14-053], which validate the bench category while serving only
their own ecosystems. The CN acceptance channel: testing houses
implementing GB 40879 and T/CIEP 0263-2025 obligations [L14-035, L14-039].

How these buyers actually purchase matters to sequencing. A cooling vendor
buys a bench when a specific customer qualification demands numbers its
in-house rig cannot certify — so the first sales motion is not a catalog
but a paid round-robin campaign attached to a live procurement, typically
scoped in weeks and paid from a program budget rather than capital
equipment. Hyperscalers and acceptance labs buy the opposite way: protocol
first, hardware second, and only after the method has published
uncertainty budgets. The milestone order below — round-robin before
protocol publication before bench catalog — follows that observed
purchasing grain rather than instrument-industry habit.

## 3. Product boundary and architecture

The product is measurement, not cooling. Architecture: (a) programmable
chip-scale thermal test vehicles — multi-kilowatt, multi-zone heater dies
that replay realistic hotspot maps rather than uniform dummy heat; (b)
rack-scale load emulators that stand in for a megawatt rack during CDU and
facility commissioning; (c) traceable calorimetry benches closing the
energy balance to reference-grade uncertainty; and (d) a published
round-robin conformance protocol with statistical acceptance criteria,
licensed to labs and vendors. Explicitly outside the boundary: cold plates,
CDUs, fluids, CFD software, and facility engineering. The moat is method
authority plus the cross-vendor round-robin dataset: once acceptance tests
cite the protocol, the bench becomes the reference artifact — a position
no single cooling vendor can occupy because none is neutral.

## 4. Feasibility

The measurement physics is mature; the product is disciplined integration.
The microchannel cold-plate literature defines the performance envelope the
bench must resolve — 206.5 W/cm² at 0.1727 K/W thermal resistance in an
optimized CPU-package cold plate [L14-009], velocity-dominant design
factors [L14-006], and a review base spanning cold plates [L14-010,
L14-003], two-phase immersion systems characterized at up to 1,168 kW
simulated load with PUE approaching 1.01 [L14-001, L14-002], single-phase
immersion test systems with measured COP 5.68-6.4 [L14-028], and the
Chinese survey literature on chiplet-era liquid cooling [L14-029]. Three
engineering sub-problems deserve honest treatment. First, programmable
heat at fidelity: a 1-2 kW die-scale TTV with 16 independently controlled
zones must hold commanded hotspot maps within tight spatial and temporal
tolerance while its own calorimetric instrumentation closes the energy
balance — a controls-and-DAQ problem squarely in the founder's stack.
Second, discrimination statistics: a round-robin is only authoritative if
lab-to-lab variance is small enough to separate real product differences;
the decisive experiment tests exactly this. Third, fluid and two-phase
qualification: the regulatory phase-down of HFCs (AIM Act step to 30% of
baseline for 2029-2033 [L14-033]) and the EU F-gas recast [L14-034] force
fluid transitions that create a second bench class — two-phase fluid
qualification loops of the kind ITRI's 2,400 W cold-plate work
foreshadows [L14-053] — sharing the same calorimetry core.

## 5. Measurable 10x edge

Against in-house dummy-load rigs, the edge is fidelity and comparability:
programmable hotspot maps versus uniform heaters, traceable calorimetric
closure versus uncalibrated power readings, and cross-lab statistical
acceptance versus single-lab numbers nobody else can reproduce. The edge
is measured in discrimination power — the smallest real performance
difference between two cold plates the method can resolve with stated
confidence. In-house rigs answer "does it work in my lab"; the product
answers "does it conform, provably, anywhere" — the difference between a
number and evidence, and it is the entire value of a conformance regime.

order book and backlog quantifying the hardware wave the method must
police [L14-048]; the Flex/JetCool acquisition and ZutaCore raise

[L14-045, L14-046]. Refresh-sensitive evidence: OCP's still-partial

re-verified as open. CN-leg evidence: GB 40879's tightening PUE limits and
the T/CIEP liquid-cooling specification create acceptance-testing

## 7. Competition

No merchant incumbent sells standards-grade liquid-cooling conformance
metrology. The adjacent players: component-level TTV suppliers (die
emulators without system calorimetry or protocol authority); in-house
rigs at every serious vendor; and the institutional analogs — NTT's
Musashino verification hub [L14-050] and ITRI's two-phase program
[L14-053] — which serve national ecosystems rather than merchant
customers. The structural competitor is not a company but a process: the
OCP community itself, the natural owner of any qualification standard
[R10-023]. That is the correct frame for strategy: the venture must shape
the method, not race it.

### 7a. Competitive scenarios and response plan

Scenario A (most likely): OCP continues publishing guidelines and white
papers without a complete method or reference hardware; the authority seat
stays open through 2028. Response: standard plan — build the bench, run
the first blind round-robin, publish the protocol. Scenario B: OCP
publishes a complete qualification methodology with reference hardware.
Response: the join-don't-race posture (a standing red-team repair) —
participate in the OCP Cooling Environments workstream from 2026 so that
the venture's TTV and calorimetry design is the reference hardware, making
an OCP publication a distribution channel rather than a kill. Scenario C:
in-house rigs remain good enough and nobody pays for neutrality. Response:
concentrate on the two wedges where neutrality is compulsory — warranty
and PUE disputes, and CN regulatory acceptance testing [L14-035, L14-039]
— and let volume follow disputes. Scenario D: the CN certification-lab
channel never converts to sales. Response: the US/JP/TW legs carry the
business; the CN acceptance regimes continue generating protocol-licensing
demand even if bench sales lag. In all scenarios the $150k experiment and
early OCP participation are the correct first moves.

## 8. Decisive 2026-2029 experiment

2026-2027 campaign: build a 1.2 kW programmable 16-zone thermal test
vehicle; demonstrate commanded hotspot-map replay with calorimetric
closure; then run a blind round-robin of three commercial cold plates
across three labs using the draft protocol. Budget: $150,000 — squarely
inside the portfolio's validation band. Thresholds: zone-level control
holding commanded maps within specification across the power range;
calorimetric energy-balance closure at reference-grade uncertainty;
round-robin variance small enough to discriminate the three plates'
real performance differences at stated confidence. KILL RULE: if the TTV
cannot hold programmable hotspot maps, or if the blind round-robin
produces non-discriminating variance (labs disagree more than products
differ), the method-authority claim is dead and the venture folds to
custom test engineering. Follow-on 2028: first paid conformance campaign
for a Deschutes-ecosystem vendor; 2029: fluid-qualification loop
prototype aligned to the HFC-transition timeline [L14-033, L14-034].

## 9. 2030-2034 timing

The window is open now — spec chaos is a 2026-2028 phenomenon — but the
demand does not expire: TDP and heat-flux trajectories [L14-010] and
cooling-technology diversity (single-phase, two-phase, immersion
[L14-001, L14-028]) outpace standards well past 2030, and CN acceptance
regimes harden with the GB 40879 revision [L14-035]. Launch 2030 rides
accrued method authority: by then the protocol has three years of
round-robin history, and the 2030-2034 phase converts authority into
fleet-scale conformance campaigns, licensed lab networks, and
fluid-transition qualification as the AIM Act's 2029-2033 step bites
[L14-033]. The monitored kill trigger — a complete OCP method with
reference hardware — is re-verified quarterly and remains untripped
[R10-023].

## 10. Independent US and China paths

US path: OCP-ecosystem participation, Deschutes-vendor round-robins
[P3R2-C-05-S02], COOLERCHIPS-lineage lab customers [L14-030], and
hyperscaler procurement enforcement [L14-043]. China path (independently
evidenced): GB 40879's PUE mandates and the T/CIEP 0263-2025 liquid-cooling
specification create acceptance-testing obligations that exist regardless
of OCP politics [L14-035, L14-039]; the channel is the CN
certification-lab ecosystem, honestly flagged as plausible-but-unconfirmed
as a sales route. The legs share instrumentation and nothing else: test
equipment of this class is uncontrolled for export in both directions, and
a dual-entity structure is trivial. Chinese-market cooling-vendor demand
(Envicool among the Deschutes eight [P3R2-C-05-S02]) bridges both legs.

## 11. Side markets

Japan: NTT's verification hub demonstrates institutional willingness to
fund exactly this bench class [L14-050]. Taiwan: ITRI's two-phase
cold-plate industrial-chain program is both a validation and a customer
channel [L14-053]. A third adjacent market is EV ultra-fast charging,
where immersion-cooled connectors at 400-800 A face the same
qualification problem [L14-022]. All optional; none substitutes for the
US/CN legs.

## 12. Regulation, safety, export controls

No certification regime governs the bench itself; the bench serves other
products' certification. The regulatory tailwinds are the AIM Act HFC
phase-down (30% of baseline for 2029-2033) [L14-033], the EU F-gas recast
[L14-034], China's GB 40879 revision [L14-035] and T/CIEP 0263-2025
[L14-039] — each converts thermal performance from marketing into
compliance, which is what makes neutral measurement sellable. Export
controls: none identified for thermal test equipment; standard lab safety
(pressurized loops, dielectric fluids) applies.

## 13. Capital and business model

v1 capital $3-8M — bench engineering, a small reference lab, and protocol
development; the $150k experiment resolves the core technical risk first.
Business model: bench sales, per-campaign round-robin and conformance
services, protocol/reference-artifact licensing to labs, and
fluid-qualification services as the transition accelerates [L14-033]. The
honest weakness is the TAM ceiling; the honest answer is that services
and licensing — not hardware — are where a method authority earns, and
the 2028-2029 milestones are built to prove or kill that thesis early.

## 15. Failure modes

(1) OCP publishes a complete method with reference hardware — the
commercial kill test; mitigated by the join-don't-race posture (scenario
B). (2) In-house rigs stay good enough — mitigated via the dispute and
regulatory-acceptance wedges (scenario C). (3) TAM ceiling: licensing
economics never materialize — surfaced early by the 2028 paid-campaign
milestone. (4) CN channel never converts — scenario D; the venture
survives on US/JP/TW. (5) Round-robin fails statistically — caught at
$150k by the kill rule.

## 15a. Residual risk assessment

The red team's decision was survive at medium residual risk with high
confidence, and its strongest counterargument — that OCP is the natural
owner of any qualification standard — is the single thought that should
discipline strategy. The response embedded in this plan: join the
workstream in 2026, aim the venture at becoming the consortium's
reference-hardware supplier, and hold the dispute/regulatory wedges where
neutrality cannot be communal. Both dominant uncertainties (OCP cadence,
licensing economics) resolve on public, observable evidence during
2027-2029, before launch capital is committed. Nothing in pack
construction changed the stage-30 verdict; the kill trigger was
re-verified as untripped [R10-023].
