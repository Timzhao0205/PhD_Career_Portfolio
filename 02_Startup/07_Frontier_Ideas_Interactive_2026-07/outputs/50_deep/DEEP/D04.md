# D04 — P3R2-D-10: Coherent-beam-combining phase-control engine

## 1. Thesis

High-energy lasers crossed from demonstration to procurement in July 2026,
and the physics dictates that everything above roughly 150 kW must be a
coherently combined array — which makes the phase-control layer the
economic chokepoint of the entire power class. FACT: on July 9, 2026 the
Pentagon awarded Joint Laser Weapon System (JLWS) agreements to nLIGHT
Defense and Lockheed Martin Aculight — $86 million initial, $847 million
combined program ceiling — for containerized 150 kW prototypes scaling to
300-500 kW against cruise missiles and drones [L12-031], with nLIGHT's own
share carrying a $627 million ceiling [L12-032] and demonstrations
targeted as early as 2028 [R10-032]. FACT: transverse mode instability is
the dominant physical limit on single-aperture fiber-laser power scaling —
a peer-reviewed theory base now models its threshold as a Hopf bifurcation
and derives semi-analytic limits under multimode excitation [L12-016,
L12-017, L12-018] — so power beyond the single-fiber ceiling comes only
from combining many apertures coherently [L12-001]. FACT: the Navy plans a
dedicated Joint Beam Control System line — $31.7 million in development
contracts as soon as Q4 2026 and $30 million for containerized hardware by
March 2027 — inside $675.93 million of Army/Navy JLWS R&D through FY2031
[P3R2-D-10-S01]. INFERENCE: a subsystem that must exist in every
300-500 kW weapon, that is procured on its own budget line, and that no
merchant vendor sells, is a classic component-monopoly opening.
HYPOTHESIS: a channel-scalable electro-optic phase-control engine — a
deterministic controller with modulator front-ends that locks 16 to 1,000+
channels to a small fraction of a wavelength — becomes the merchant
"conductor" of coherent light for weapons primes, ultrafast-laser OEMs,
and beamed-power systems alike.

## 2. Customer job and named buyers

The customer job is phase discipline at scale: hold every channel of a
combined array locked to a common wavefront within tens of nanometers,
at control bandwidths that ride through platform vibration and thermal
drift, with graceful degradation when channels fail, and with enough
determinism to certify. Named buyers on the weapons leg: the two JLWS
primes — nLIGHT Defense, whose coherent-beam-combining lineage runs back
to the 2019 HELSI program [P3R2-D-10-S01], and Lockheed Martin Aculight
[L12-031] — plus the Navy's JBCS program office as the direct
subsystem customer [P3R2-D-10-S01]. Named buyers on the science and
non-weapons government leg: DARPA's MELT program, whose Phase 2 tasked
Northrop Grumman with a coherently combined semiconductor laser tile
array scalable from kilowatts toward megawatts [L12-041, R10-041];
DARPA's AMPED chip-scale high-power laser program [L12-040]; the NNSA
Enhanced Yield Capability upgrade at NIF (2.2 to 2.6 MJ, CD-1 approved
April 2026, GAO-estimated $470M-$1B total cost) as the
precision-phase-control analog at facility scale [L12-042]; and DARPA's
POWER beamed-energy program, which delivered 800 W of optical power over
8.6 km in May 2025 [L12-047]. Named buyers on the industrial leg:
ultrafast OEMs pursuing combined femtosecond systems of the class the
literature already demonstrates — 403 W at 260 fs from two coherently
combined channels with M² < 1.2 [L12-002] — inside a merchant fiber-laser
market whose incumbents (IPG at $1.0 billion revenue, nLIGHT's laser
products at $179 million) are locked in a power-and-price race with
Chinese rivals [L12-034, L12-033].

## 3. Product boundary and architecture

The product is the control layer, not the laser. Architecture: a
deterministic FPGA-class controller (migrating to ASIC at volume) running
stochastic-parallel-gradient-descent and interferometric phase-locking
algorithms; electro-optic modulator front-end boards per channel; phase
pickoff and metrology optics; and a certification-grade telemetry and
fault-management layer. Sold as a brassboard subsystem, a licensed
reference design, and eventually a qualified line-replaceable unit.
Explicitly outside the boundary: gain fibers, amplifiers, beam directors,
and adaptive-optics atmospheric correction — the last is a distinct
discipline with its own literature [L12-009, L12-010, L12-011] and its
own primes; the product interfaces with AO, it does not replace it. The
moat is channel-count scalability with certified determinism: going from
16 to 1,000+ channels without re-architecting is the hard, compounding
engineering asset.

## 4. Feasibility

Phase locking of combined arrays is demonstrated physics at laboratory
scale; the venture is in the scaling discipline. The tiled-aperture CBC
literature establishes the architecture and its beam-shaping headroom
[L12-001]; the ultrafast demonstration at 403 W/260 fs proves coherent
combination survives dispersion-managed femtosecond operation [L12-002];
the TMI theory base defines exactly why the market must combine rather
than scale single apertures [L12-016, L12-017, L12-018]. Three
engineering sub-problems deserve honest treatment. First, control-loop
scaling: SPGD convergence time degrades with channel count unless the
controller exploits parallel metrology and hierarchical loop structure;
the deterministic sub-microsecond loop at 1,000 channels is a hardware
architecture problem — the founder's home ground — not new physics.
Second, vibration environments: a containerized weapon sees continuous
platform disturbance; the 2027 brassboard therefore locks under 1g
vibration, not on an optical table. Third, integration against AO: the
phase engine must hand off cleanly to atmospheric-correction layers
[L12-009, L12-010] without control-loop interference — an interface
specification issue best solved early with a government lab partner of
the Apollon-class AO community's sophistication [L12-011].

## 5. Measurable 10x edge

Channel-scalable λ/30 rms phase lock with >90% combining efficiency,
maintained under vibration and scaled from 16 toward 1,000+ channels, is
the enabling step for the entire 300-500 kW class — there is no
alternative path to those powers within single-aperture physics [L12-016].
Against in-house prime solutions, the edge is specialization economics: a
merchant engine amortized across weapons, industrial ultrafast, beamed
power, and facility-scale programs versus one-program point solutions
rebuilt inside each prime. The metric set is unambiguous and benchable:
residual phase error, combining efficiency, lock acquisition time,
channel-failure ride-through, and control latency.

## 6. Demand

Durable demand evidence: the executed JLWS awards and their $847 million
combined ceiling [L12-031, L12-032, R10-032]; the dedicated Navy
beam-control budget line and $675.93 million of JLWS R&D through FY2031
[P3R2-D-10-S01]; DARPA's MELT and AMPED programs betting on combined and
chip-scale architectures [L12-041, L12-040]; roughly $1 billion per year
of DOD directed-energy development spending documented by GAO — along
with the "valley of death" transition problem that makes subsystem
maturity a procurement criterion [L12-045]; and the NNSA EYC program as
a non-weapons precision-laser demand pole [L12-042]. Industrial-leg
evidence: IPG's 10-K names Wuhan Raycus explicitly and describes a
power-scaling price war [L12-034] — coherent combining of modest,
cheap apertures is the cost-curve answer, and the 403 W ultrafast result
shows the technical path [L12-002]. International context confirming the
category (not addressable markets): Korea's fielded Cheongwang laser
weapon with its newly localized oscillator [P3R2-D-10-S02], Israel's
Iron Beam lineage moving to airborne platforms [P3R2-D-10-S03], and
China's portable Lijian systems already deployed at domestic airports
[P3R2-D-10-S04].

Bottom-up arithmetic (every input labeled). FACT: JLWS targets 150 kW
scaling to 300-500 kW [L12-031]. ASSUMPTION: combined architectures at
those powers require on the order of 100-1,000 channels (from published
kW-class channel powers in the CBC literature [L12-001, L12-002]).
ASSUMPTION: phase-control content (controller + per-channel front-ends)
is worth $2-10k per channel at defense-qualified pricing — no public
price source exists; explicitly an open gap. INFERENCE: each fielded
300-500 kW system carries $1-5M of phase-control content; a
several-dozen-system 2030s fleet plus industrial and beamed-power
adoption is a $50-250M cumulative opportunity. The load-bearing
conclusion is narrower: one design win inside JBCS/JLWS funds the
company; the arithmetic only needs the socket to exist, which the budget
lines prove [P3R2-D-10-S01].

## 7. Competition

There is no merchant phase-control vendor — the 2026-07 verification
pass confirmed the competition is prime-internal teams (nLIGHT, LM
Aculight) and program-funded lab efforts [L12-031, R10-041]. The
structural threat is internalization: beam control is crown-jewel
territory, and the red team's strongest counterargument stands — primes
may no more outsource it than flight-control laws. The counter-structure:
the Navy is budgeting beam control as its own line item [P3R2-D-10-S01],
which is exactly the procurement shape that creates merchant
subsystem slots; and the industrial ultrafast leg [L12-002, L12-034]
gives the venture a non-defense revenue base no prime will contest.

### 7a. Competitive scenarios and response plan

Scenario A (most likely): primes keep system integration, JBCS carves
out subsystem development slots, and SBIR/OTA pathways admit component
vendors. Response: standard plan — brassboard, SBIR position, prime
teaming during 2026-2027 (a standing red-team repair, pulled ahead of
the nominal launch date). Scenario B: primes classify and internalize
beam control entirely; JBCS awards go exclusively to JLWS primes.
Response: the industrial ultrafast and beamed-power legs [L12-002,
L12-047] become primary; the venture sells combining engines to laser
OEMs fighting the price war [L12-034], and re-enters defense as a
qualified second source when transition pressure [L12-045] forces cost
discipline. Scenario C: Civan-class integrated CBC approaches bypass the
merchant controller by fusing modulation into the amplifier chain.
Response: compete at the metrology-and-algorithm layer where integration
approaches still need lock authority, and hold the >100-channel regime
where integrated approaches have not demonstrated. Scenario D: DE
budgets contract after a failed intercept era. Response: NNSA facility
demand [L12-042] and industrial combining are budget-uncorrelated; the
company shrinks to profitability rather than dying.

## 8. Decisive 2026-2029 experiment

2027 brassboard: a 16-channel fiber array phase-locked to λ/30 rms
residual error with >90% combining efficiency, maintained under 1g
random vibration, with deterministic lock acquisition and
channel-dropout ride-through. Budget: $250,000. Thresholds: λ/30 rms
across the band; >90% combining efficiency; lock reacquisition within
specification after seeded channel failure; all under vibration. KILL
RULE: failing λ/30 or 90% efficiency under vibration kills the
scalability claim and the venture folds to component licensing
(modulator front-ends only). Follow-on 2028: 64-channel scaling
demonstration plus an SBIR/JBCS teaming position; 2029: industrial
ultrafast design win with an OEM [L12-002-class architecture].

## 9. 2030-2034 timing

The awkward truth, stated plainly at P4 and preserved here: the
design-win window opens 2026-2028, earlier than the 2030 launch
convention. The plan is built for that reality — SBIR/teaming positions
and the brassboard occupy 2026-2029 as pre-company activity, so the
company launches in 2030 into JLWS's 300-500 kW scale-up and fielding
decisions, which run through FY2031 and beyond [P3R2-D-10-S01, L12-031].
Adoption 2030-2034: qualified LRU status inside one weapons program;
two-plus industrial OEM design wins; beamed-power demonstrations as
POWER-lineage programs mature [L12-047]. The GAO transition problem
[L12-045] is the macro risk — prototypes that never become programs —
and the industrial leg is the hedge.

## 10. Independent US and China paths

This is the portfolio's one deliberately single-market idea. US path:
everything above. China path: none, by design — ITAR governs the
weapons leg from day one, and the venture is structured as a US entity
with a segregated dual-use industrial line; no Chinese beachhead exists,
and the P4 geography score honestly reflects it. China appears in this
idea only as context: deployed portable systems [P3R2-D-10-S04] and the
IPG/Raycus industrial rivalry [L12-034] confirm the technology class
matters there, but that market is closed to this venture and is
excluded from every revenue assumption. The portfolio carries its
US/China balance at the portfolio level, not by forcing a CN leg here.

## 11. Side markets

Korea and Israel are category validators and potential FMS-mediated
integration partners, not direct markets: Korea fields Cheongwang and is
localizing components upward [P3R2-D-10-S02]; Israel is pushing lasers
onto airborne platforms [P3R2-D-10-S03]. The genuine side market is
industrial: ultrafast combining for machining OEMs [L12-002] and
optical power beaming [L12-047], both ITAR-separable through the
segregated line.

## 12. Regulation, safety, export controls

ITAR is the dominant constraint and is treated as architecture, not
afterthought: US-person engineering, segregated facilities for the
dual-use industrial line, and export classification review before any
foreign OEM engagement. Laser safety at combined powers is standard
industrial-laser discipline. The AMPED dollar figures remain
single-source and are not load-bearing anywhere in this plan [L12-040];
the FY2027 directed-energy budget top-line was snippet-grade at red-team
time and is likewise not load-bearing — the executed JLWS awards and
the JBCS line items carry the demand case alone [L12-031, L12-032,
P3R2-D-10-S01].

## 13. Capital and business model

v1 capital $3-8M: brassboard-to-64-channel engineering, a small
qualified-manufacturing cell for front-end boards, and clearance/ITAR
overhead — the last is real money and is why the floor is not $2M.
Business model: development contracts (SBIR/JBCS), then qualified
subsystem sales at defense margins, then industrial OEM licensing; the
ASIC migration at volume is the gross-margin inflection. Development
revenue arrives unusually early for hardware — SBIR and study contracts
pay during 2027-2028, before the company exists formally — which is why
the equity requirement stays in the single-digit millions despite the
defense setting. The $250k experiment resolves the core scalability
claim before any of that capital is committed.

## 14. Founder skill transfer

Direct fit: deterministic FPGA control loops, electro-optic drive
electronics, interferometric metrology, and high-channel-count signal
processing are the founder's core stack; SPGD and phase-locking
algorithms are numerical-methods territory. The gap is defense
program-capture experience — addressed through the teaming posture
rather than hiring ahead of revenue.

## 15. Failure modes

(1) Prime internalization with no JBCS carve-out — the commercial kill
test; hedged by the industrial leg (scenario B). (2) Window timing: the
design-win contest resolves 2026-2028 before launch — met by doing the
teaming now, as pre-company work. (3) ITAR overhead sinking a
components-scale startup — priced into the v1 floor. (4) Integrated-CBC
bypass (scenario C). (5) DE budget winter (scenario D). Each failure
mode has a named, dated observable; none is a silent killer.

## 15a. Residual risk assessment

Red-team decision: survive, medium residual risk, high confidence. The
strongest counterargument — beam control as crown-jewel, never
outsourced — is respected rather than argued away: the plan's center of
gravity is winning a position inside the procurement the Navy has
already separated out [P3R2-D-10-S01], and its fallback is a real
industrial market where no classification pressure exists [L12-034,
L12-002]. The decisive uncertainties (JBCS award shape, prime posture)
resolve on public contract actions during 2026-2028 — before launch
capital, and mostly before the brassboard is even complete.
