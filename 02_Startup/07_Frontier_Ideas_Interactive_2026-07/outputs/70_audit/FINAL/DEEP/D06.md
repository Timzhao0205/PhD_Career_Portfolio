# D06 — P3R2-A-14: 300 °C mixed-signal instrumentation platform for superhot environments

## 1. Thesis

The hottest places money now wants to go are beyond the temperature
ceiling of every purchasable electronics platform, and the one merchant
line that came closest has just left the market. FACT: commercial
high-temperature practice tops out around 175-225 °C — TI-class parts
stop at 175-210 °C, and CISSOID's CHT family (rated to 225 °C, 5-year
life) has gone to last-time-buy after X-FAB terminated the XI10 SOI
process it was built on; the ordering window closed in mid-2025
[L15-043, P3R2-A-14-S04]. FACT: the buyers are heading to 300 °C and
beyond — ARPA-E's SUPERHOT program targets reservoirs above 375 °C and
22 MPa with a 15-year well life [L15-029, P3R2-A-14-S02]; Baker Hughes
and XGS Energy committed in March 2026 to a 150 MW New Mexico geothermal
project feeding Meta datacenters via PNM, full operation targeted 2030
[P3R2-A-14-S01]; SLB and Ormat are appraising an EGS pilot at Desert
Peak, Nevada, explicitly citing datacenter power demand [L15-041,
P3R2-A-14-S06, R10-011]; and Quaise closed a $134M Series B tranche to
drill into 300-500 °C rock, first test well from July 2026 [R10-010].
FACT: the device physics is proven — SiC devices reach junction
temperatures near 600 °C in theory and 727 °C in lab prototypes
[L15-001], SOI CMOS platforms characterize to 300 °C [L15-004], and a
SiC JFET IC has run for prolonged periods in a simulated 460 °C Venus
surface environment [L15-009]. INFERENCE: between what is purchasable
(225 °C, now discontinued) and what is required (300 °C+, dated to
2030 projects) sits a live, realized sourcing gap. HYPOTHESIS: a
merchant, qualified 300 °C mixed-signal instrumentation platform —
chipset plus qualified packaging plus module — becomes the standard
sensing-and-telemetry electronics of superhot geothermal, deep
downhole, and turbine environments, with an 800 °C tier behind it.

## 2. Customer job and named buyers

The customer job is measurement where silicon dies: pressure,
temperature, vibration, and flow telemetry from tool strings, wellheads
and hot sections, sampled with mixed-signal precision, surviving not
minutes but thousands of hours. Named buyers, commercial: Baker Hughes
— $600M FY2025 R&D, $2.0B of New Energy orders including a named
five-plant ORC geothermal equipment award [L15-040] — and its XGS
project organization [P3R2-A-14-S01]; SLB/Ormat's EGS program
[L15-041, P3R2-A-14-S06]; Quaise and Mazama as superhot drillers with
funded programs [R10-010, P3R2-A-14-S03]. Named buyers, government
program offices: DOE FORGE's awardee ecosystem (Welltec, PetroQuip,
Fervo, national labs) buying 225 °C-class zonal-isolation and packer
tooling today [L15-027, L15-028]; ARPA-E SUPERHOT performers needing
well-construction instrumentation above 375 °C [L15-029]; DARPA's
THERMAL program soliciting 800 °C mixed-signal ICs with geothermal
monitoring named as an application [L15-026, P3R2-A-14-S05]; NASA's
HOTTech Venus-surface program and its GEER test facility [L15-030]. The
historical proof that this buyer class pays for merchant HT platforms:
DOE's Deep Trek program funded Honeywell to build >225 °C SOI EEPROM,
FPGA and ADC components for smart drilling two decades ago [L15-031].

## 3. Product boundary and architecture

The product is a qualified platform, not a foundry and not a tool
company: (a) a 300 °C mixed-signal chipset — instrumentation
amplifiers, ADCs, references, multiplexed front-ends — on SiC and SOI
processes chosen per function [L15-001, L15-004]; (b) the qualified
packaging stack — sintered-silver die-attach and matched interconnect
rated for 200-300 °C interfaces [L15-005] — which is the true gate and
is treated as a first-class product asset, not a vendor problem; and
(c) rugged instrumentation modules (8-16 channel front-ends) sold to
tool builders. Explicitly outside the boundary: drilling tools,
completions hardware, and sensors themselves. The distinction against
the one active merchant competitor is precise: Ozark IC sells
instrumentation nodes and boards (its XNode spans -180 °C to 800 °C in
demonstrations, with a $1.15M DOE geothermal award through September
2026, per this project's stage-30 verification); this venture sells the
qualified chipset-plus-packaging platform beneath that product class —
the layer whose absence CISSOID's line closure just exposed
[P3R2-A-14-S04].

## 4. Feasibility

Device feasibility is literature-settled; the venture lives or dies on
qualification discipline. The SiC review base names the four honest
bottlenecks — gate-drive design, high-temperature current sensing,
cross-temperature parameter matching, packaging maturity [L15-001] —
and the SOI path to 300 °C requires derated current density against
electromigration [L15-004]. Three engineering sub-problems deserve
honest treatment. First, packaging as the gate: die-attach and
interconnect reliability at 300 °C is empirically qualified, not
designed on paper — the sintered-silver literature defines the test
regime (high-temperature storage, power cycling, thermal shock)
[L15-005], and SiC power-cycling reliability work defines the metrics
[L15-025]; the decisive experiment's 500-thermal-cycle leg exists
because this, not the silicon, is where the plan can fail — and it is
honestly outside the founder's home profile, which is why the first
technical hire is a packaging engineer, not a circuit designer. Second,
drift: <1% calibrated drift over 1,000 hours at 300 °C is the
qualification claim buyers actually purchase; parameter matching across
temperature [L15-001] is the circuit-design core. Third, environments
beyond heat: downhole and nuclear-adjacent deployments add radiation
and corrosive-atmosphere requirements — the Venus-environment SiC
demonstration [L15-009] and the Fukushima robot component irradiation
campaign [L15-011] bracket the qualification envelope the platform
should eventually claim.

## 5. Measurable 10x edge

300 °C continuous, qualified operation is not an incremental spec — it
is a capability class no commercial vendor sells. The purchasable
ceiling is 225 °C with five-year life, and that line is in last-time-buy
[L15-043, P3R2-A-14-S04]; in-house oilfield electronics teams stop
around 200 °C (stage-20 competitive finding). The edge is measured in
qualification currency: rated hours at temperature, drift under
calibration, thermal-cycle count on the packaging stack — numbers that
gate whether a $100M well program can log data at all. Behind it sits a
second tier the DARPA THERMAL targets define: 800 °C mixed-signal
operation [L15-026], where the venture's SiC platform road-maps while
competitors' SOI paths cannot follow [L15-001, L15-004].

## 7. Competition

Restated with the precision the red team required. CISSOID the company
remains active in SiC automotive power; it is specifically the
high-temperature mixed-signal CHT line that closed with X-FAB's process
termination [P3R2-A-14-S04, L15-043]. Ozark IC is an active merchant
competitor — funded by DOE geothermal money and demonstrating
instrument nodes across extreme ranges (stage-30 verification) — and
the geothermal-instrumentation beachhead is therefore not empty; the
contested boundary is platform-versus-instrument, and the venture's
claim is the qualified chipset/packaging layer. TI-class incumbents cap
at 175-210 °C. Oilfield in-house teams run to ~200 °C and could extend.
DARPA THERMAL performers are the funded future rivals above 300 °C —
and the correct posture is co-option: become the packaging and
qualification platform their 800 °C dies need [L15-026].

### 7a. Competitive scenarios and response plan

Scenario A (most likely): the superhot programs stay on their 2030
schedules, in-house teams stay at 200 °C, and Ozark grows in
instruments while the platform layer stays open. Response: standard
plan. Scenario B: SLB or Baker Hughes announces an internal 300 °C
platform — the red team's strongest counterargument made real.
Response: sell the packaging/qualification layer to the majors' own
programs (they buy die-attach reliability regardless of whose die), and
concentrate on the multi-vendor program market the majors will not
serve (FORGE/SUPERHOT awardees, labs, space [L15-027, L15-029,
L15-030]). Scenario C: Ozark wins the named superhot instrumentation
sockets first. Response: platform-supply position — Ozark-class
instruments are prospective customers for qualified 300 °C chipsets,
and the venture's economics survive as the Intel-inside of HT
instrumentation; direct instrument competition resumes only where the
platform position fails. Scenario D: superhot drilling slips wholesale
past 2030. Response: the market stays program-funded — which still
pays: FORGE/SUPERHOT/THERMAL cadence [L15-027, L15-028, L15-026] plus
aero and space tiers [L15-030] carry a smaller company to the delayed
window, exactly as Deep Trek carried Honeywell's platform through a
pre-commercial decade [L15-031].

## 8. Decisive 2026-2029 experiment

Staged 2026-2028 campaign, $850k total — above the portfolio's
preferred band, and structured so the spend gates itself. Stage one
(2026-2027, ~$250k): packaging first — sintered-silver die-attach and
interconnect stack through 500 thermal cycles to 300 °C with
post-cycle shear and electrical integrity [L15-005, L15-025]. KILL
GATE ONE: a packaging stack that cannot pass 500 cycles kills the
platform before any silicon money is spent. Stage two (2027-2028,
~$600k): an 8-channel SiC/SOI instrumentation front-end operated 1,000
hours at 300 °C with <1% calibrated drift [L15-001, L15-004]. KILL
GATE TWO: failing drift or lifetime kills the qualification claim and
folds the venture to packaging services. Commercial milestone bound to
the same window (red-team dated repair): one named program — a
Quaise, Mazama, or FORGE awardee — converted into a paid
instrumentation pilot by 2028 [R10-010, P3R2-A-14-S03, L15-027].

## 9. 2030-2034 timing

Preparation 2026-2029 is qualification-first, matching how this market
buys: nobody integrates unqualified electronics into a $100M well. The
2030 launch lands on the dated commercial triggers — BH/XGS full
operation [P3R2-A-14-S01], Quaise's Project Obsidian [R10-010] — with
DARPA THERMAL Phase II maturing the 800 °C tier on roughly the same
clock [L15-026]. Adoption 2030-2034: tool-builder design-ins across
two-plus superhot programs; aero-engine hot-section instrumentation as
the second leg; space qualification (HOTTech-lineage Venus and
inner-system missions [L15-030, L15-009]) as the decade's option value.
If the superhot wave slips, the program-funded bridge holds the
platform at smaller scale — the honest downside is slower, not dead.

## 10. Independent US and China paths

US-only, verified twice: stage-20 screening and a stage-30 direct
bilingual search re-confirmed no Chinese demand pole or supply channel
for merchant 300 °C mixed-signal platforms — no CN beachhead is
claimed, no CN revenue is assumed, and the portfolio's dual-market
balance is carried elsewhere. The US leg stands on named domestic
buyers and federal programs alone [P3R2-A-14-S01, L15-027, L15-029,
L15-026]. Export posture: no control regime specific to HT downhole
instrumentation was identified; the space and defense-adjacent tiers
(THERMAL, HOTTech) are US-government programs executed domestically.

## 11. Side markets

Japan is the one genuine side channel: the Fukushima decommissioning
robotics ecosystem qualifies electronics for combined
radiation-and-temperature environments [L15-011], a demand profile the
platform's qualification envelope can grow into. Aero-engine
instrumentation (US/EU primes) and Venus-class space missions
[L15-030] are platform extensions rather than geographic side markets.

## 12. Regulation, safety, export controls

Qualification is industrial (customer and program acceptance), not
regulatory: the relevant regimes are program qualification standards
(NASA GEER-class environmental test protocols [L15-030], DOE program
milestones) rather than certification law. No export-control trigger
identified for the geothermal/downhole tiers; any future
defense-adjacent 800 °C work under THERMAL lineage [L15-026] would
carry standard government-contract data controls handled inside a US
entity. Lab safety is conventional high-temperature test practice.

## 13. Capital and business model

v1 capital $5-12M: two qualification campaigns, a packaging line
partnership, and module productization. The staged $850k experiment is
the largest pre-company spend in the top ten and is gated so that
packaging failure costs $250k, not $850k. Business model: qualified
chipset and module sales to tool builders, packaging/qualification
services to program performers (revenue during the bridge years), and
the 800 °C tier as the future premium line — with the adjacent
power-conversion tier (gate drivers, HT DC-DC of the class the
MV-to-LVDC literature road-maps [L02-001]) as the second product family
once the instrumentation platform earns its qualification history.

## 14. Founder skill transfer

Honest split: mixed-signal front-ends, DAQ architecture, calibration
and drift analysis are core founder strengths; high-temperature
packaging is not, and the plan says so — the packaging engineer is
hire one, and stage one of the experiment exists partly to force that
discipline early. Program-capture in the DOE/ARPA-E world is learnable
and is why the paid-pilot milestone is dated 2028, not 2030.

## 15. Failure modes

(1) Superhot slip past 2030 — the market stays program-funded;
scenario D absorbs it. (2) Packaging failure — kill gate one, $250k.
(3) In-house capture by SLB/BH — scenario B converts it to a
customer relationship at the packaging layer. (4) Ozark or THERMAL
performers take the sockets — scenario C's platform-supply posture.
(5) The 2030 buyers demand flight heritage the venture lacks —
mitigated by placing modules in FORGE-class R&D wells during
2028-2029, which is what the paid-pilot milestone is for [L15-027].

## 15a. Residual risk assessment

Red-team decision: survive, medium residual risk, high confidence —
with the sharpest restatements in the portfolio (CISSOID precision,
Ozark's active status), both incorporated above. The two dominant
uncertainties are the superhot schedule and the packaging gate; the
first resolves on public drilling news through 2027-2028 [R10-010],
the second resolves for $250k inside the venture's own first year.
Neither requires launch capital to find out. The idea's honest
character: the least crowded technical frontier in the top ten,
purchased with the portfolio's most expensive experiment — the staging
is what makes that trade acceptable.
