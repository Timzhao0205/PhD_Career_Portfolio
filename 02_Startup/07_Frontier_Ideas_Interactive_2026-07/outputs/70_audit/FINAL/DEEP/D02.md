# D02 — P3R2-C-01: Certified merchant protection layer for 800 VDC AI racks

## 1. Thesis

The AI datacenter power train is converting from AC distribution to
DC-native architecture on a platform-owner schedule, and the safety layer of
that architecture is documented — by the buyers themselves — as unbuilt.
FACT: NVIDIA specifies 800 VDC architecture for megawatt-scale racks from
2027 (Kyber/Rubin Ultra), claiming up to 5% end-to-end efficiency gain, a
45% reduction in copper, up to 70% lower maintenance cost and roughly 30%
lower TCO, with Analog Devices, Infineon, TI, STMicroelectronics, Eaton,
Schneider Electric and Vertiv named as ecosystem partners [L02-043]. FACT:
the OCP "Mount Diablo" initiative (Google, Meta, Microsoft) separates
compute and power into a sidecar rack, targeting 400 VDC now and 800/1500
VDC next, and warns that future AI racks need up to 1 MW against roughly
120 kW today [L02-044]. FACT: China's OAII 800V forum (April 2026,
co-chaired by Baidu and ByteDance with Huawei, Vertiv, Schneider and Inspur
participating) states that 800 V DC protection logic differs completely
from legacy 240 V HVDC practice and that China lacks unified fault-output,
grounding, arc and insulation-monitoring standards and any 800 V
safety-certification standard [P3R2-C-01-S03]. INFERENCE: every DC-native
rack needs a protection element that clears faults faster than
semiconductors fail, and no incumbent product category — AC breakers,
fuses, or grid-scale DC breakers — occupies that socket. HYPOTHESIS: a
certified merchant protection shelf — solid-state breaker, precharge and
inrush control, insulation monitoring, and hot-swap sequencing in one
embeddable unit — becomes the safety-critical layer that every power-shelf
vendor embeds rather than builds, on both the US and Chinese legs of the
transition.

## 2. Customer job and named buyers

The customer job is to make an 800 VDC rack safe to energize, safe to
service, and safe to fail: interrupt a busbar fault in microseconds,
discriminate a real arc from a megawatt load transient without nuisance
trips, precharge capacitor banks without inrush damage, monitor insulation
continuously, and sequence hot-swap so a technician can pull a module from
a live 800 V shelf. Named buyers on the US leg: the power-shelf and sidecar
vendors NVIDIA itself names — Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider Electric [L02-043]
— plus Delta, whose 800 VDC In-Row Power rack and megawatt-class CDUs for
the NVIDIA MGX architecture were announced in May 2026 [L02-037], with the
OCP hyperscalers (Google, Meta, Microsoft) setting the sidecar
specification the shelf must live in [L02-044]. Named buyers on the CN leg:
the OAII buyer coalition itself (Baidu, ByteDance, with Huawei and Inspur)
[P3R2-C-01-S03]; the Alibaba HVDC lineage running from the 2019
Delta/Alibaba "Panama" supply through Autrans' disclosed deliveries to
Alibaba datacenters and East-Data-West-Computing parks [L02-048, L02-049];
and the 31-company domestic 800 VDC chain (Sungrow's AIDC division, Kehua
serving Tencent/ByteDance/Alibaba) that will need a protection layer it can
certify [R10-019].

## 3. Product boundary and architecture

The product is a certified, embeddable protection shelf, not switchgear and
not a power converter. Architecture: a hybrid solid-state circuit breaker
(SiC main path with mechanical isolation, the topology family demonstrated
in the SSCB literature [L08-017, L08-018]), an arc-fault discrimination
engine drawing on the DC arc-detection methods now published for
photovoltaic and DC distribution systems [L08-019, L08-020, L08-021],
precharge/inrush sequencing, continuous insulation monitoring (IMD), and
hot-swap interlock logic — sold as a shelf and as a licensed reference
design to power-shelf vendors. Explicitly outside the boundary: solid-state
transformers, rectifier shelves, busway, and grid-scale DC breakers of the
Hitachi Energy class [L08-052]. The moat is certification plus embed
position: a protection shelf certified against IEC 62477-1 (the safety
standard for power electronic converter systems up to 1500 VDC [L02-112])
that shelf vendors can drop in is cheaper for every vendor than each
running its own certification campaign.

## 4. Feasibility

The physics is demonstrated; the integration and certification are the
venture. Solid-state DC interruption with millisecond self-healing is
published for MVDC distribution with datacenter application named
[L08-017]; a tested 400 V SiC SSCB prototype with drive, fault-detection,
energy-absorption and snubber circuits exists in the literature [L08-018];
hybrid breaker topologies and their stress-handling limits are
characterized at grid scale [L08-001, L08-003]; wavelet-based DC fault
discrimination is demonstrated on multi-terminal DC systems [L08-004].
Three engineering sub-problems deserve honest treatment. First, energy
absorption at rack fault energies: an 800 V bus feeding megawatt racks
stores serious inductive energy, and the snubber/MOV design that absorbs it
in under 100 microseconds without destroying the semiconductors is the core
hardware problem — bounded by the thyristor-FCL and hybrid-breaker stress
literature [L08-003, L08-001]. Second, discrimination: separating a genuine
series arc from the transient signature of a 1 MW load step is a
signal-processing problem the PV arc-detection literature attacks with
learned models [L08-019, L08-020] and morphological methods [L08-021]; the
rack environment is actually more favorable because load profiles are
known. Third, reliability interactions: the DC-link capacitor banks the
shelf precharges are themselves the converter reliability bottleneck
[L02-010], so the shelf's telemetry (leakage trend, precharge time drift)
becomes a condition-monitoring product as well as a safety product.

## 5. Measurable 10x edge

Mechanical DC breakers and fuses clear in milliseconds; semiconductor
failure happens in microseconds. Sub-100-microsecond bidirectional clearing
at 800 V rack current — the brassboard target — is an order-of-magnitude
protection-speed step over incumbent practice, and the difference is not
incremental: it is the difference between a contained module failure and a
cascading busbar event. The second edge axis is integration density: one
certified shelf replacing a breaker, a precharge circuit, an IMD and
hot-swap interlocks engineered separately by every vendor. The edge is
measurable on a bench: clearing time, let-through energy, nuisance-trip
rate under recorded 1 MW-class load transients, and hot-swap cycle count.

## 6. Demand

Durable demand evidence: the platform owner's own architecture documents
name protection and serviceability among the open gaps and quantify the
economic pull (5% efficiency, 45% copper, ~30% TCO) [L02-043]; the OCP
hyperscalers have published the sidecar architecture the shelf slots into
[L02-044]; GE Vernova booked $2.4 billion of datacenter electrification
orders in Q1 2026 alone — exceeding its full-year 2025 total — inside a
$7.1 billion, +86% year-on-year electrification order book [L08-033];
Wolfspeed reports ~30% sequential AI-datacenter revenue growth on the
400-to-800 V transition [L02-047]. On the CN leg: the OAII forum's
documented admission that the protection and certification layer does not
exist [P3R2-C-01-S03]; the state AI-energy action plan (April 2026)
formalizing AI-datacenter energy infrastructure as national policy
[L14-036]; and the device-level ecosystem already committed — Innoscience
as the sole mainland chip company in NVIDIA's 800 V supplier list, with an
all-GaN 800V-to-1V solution for Kyber [L02-050, P3R2-C-01-S01, R10-021].

Bottom-up arithmetic (every input labeled). FACT: current high-density
racks run about 120 kW and the architecture targets up to 1 MW per rack
[L02-044]. ASSUMPTION: each 800 VDC rack or power sidecar carries one
protection shelf, and shelf pricing lands at $5,000-15,000 (certified
safety-subsystem analogy; no direct price source exists — an explicitly
open gap). ASSUMPTION: hyperscaler 800 VDC rack deployments reach the low
tens of thousands of units per year by 2030-2032 if the Kyber-generation
ramp [P3R2-C-01-S06] follows the order-book trajectory visible in
electrification bookings [L08-033]. INFERENCE: the protection-layer
hardware opportunity is in the $100-500M/yr class by the early 2030s,
before licensing and fleet-telemetry layers. This is decision arithmetic,
not a market forecast; the load-bearing conclusion is only that the socket
is large enough to carry a company if the certification position is won.

## 7. Competition

The socket is contested but not taken. FACT: the funded competitive set
includes DG Matrix (inside the NVIDIA MGX reference design), Amperesand,
Heron Power, Novos Power, Eaton (which acquired Resilient Power Systems in
August 2025), ABB's SACE Infinitus and LS Electric, with more than $320M
invested in SST startups in the twelve months to March 2026
[P3R2-C-01-S04]. Most of that capital is aimed at the conversion layer
(solid-state transformers and power shelves), not the protection shelf;
ABB's SACE Infinitus — IEC-certified first in the maritime segment, per
this project's stage-30 verification — is positioned but not yet qualified
for the 800 VDC datacenter socket. Device vendors (Navitas GaN/SiC modules
[L02-046], ROHM's 750 V SiC parts in AI-server BBUs [L02-051]) supply
components, not certified protection systems. The Chinese technical press
frames devices, DC/DC modules and cooling as the opportunity areas and
does not name a certified 800 V protection vendor [L02-054, R10-019]. The
stage-30 caveat stands: DG Matrix's shipping/certification status rests on
snippet-grade sources and must be re-verified directly each quarter.

### 7a. Competitive scenarios and response plan

Scenario A (most likely): the reference architecture absorbs conversion
(SST/shelf vendors win that layer) while protection stays fragmented per
vendor; the certified merchant shelf socket stays open into 2028. Response:
standard plan — brassboard, early UL/IEC filing, embed deals. Scenario B:
DG Matrix or ABB lands a certified 800 VDC protection product inside the
reference ecosystem by 2027. Response: compete on embed economics and CN
leg — a maritime- or SST-derived product is not an embeddable shelf, and
neither competitor has a Chinese certification path; if the US socket
closes entirely, the CN standard vacuum [P3R2-C-01-S03] still offers a
first-certified position. Scenario C: hyperscalers vertically integrate
protection into OCP sidecar designs and publish it openly [L02-044].
Response: pivot from product to certified-implementation licensing —
an open reference design still needs a certified manufacturer of record,
and the arc-discrimination dataset does not commoditize with a schematic.
Scenario D: China issues an 800 V safety standard rapidly and reserves
certification for domestic vendors. Response: the licensed CN entity with
domestic GaN supply [L02-050] carries the CN leg; the US leg is unaffected.
The $350k brassboard and early certification filing are correct first
moves in all four scenarios.

## 8. Decisive 2026-2029 experiment

Q2-2027 brassboard: an 800 V / 250 A hybrid solid-state breaker shelf
demonstrating sub-100-microsecond bidirectional clearing, energy absorption
at inductive rack-fault energies, 10,000 hot-swap cycles, and arc
discrimination with zero nuisance trips against a recorded library of
megawatt-class load transients. Budget: $350,000. Thresholds: clearing
<100 µs at 800 V/250 A in both directions; discrimination AUC sufficient
for zero false trips on the transient library while catching 100% of seeded
arc signatures [L08-019, L08-021]; 10,000 mechanical hot-swap cycles
without interlock failure. KILL RULE: if bidirectional clearing cannot
reach 100 µs at rated current, or arc discrimination cannot reach zero
nuisance trips on realistic transients, the merchant-shelf thesis fails and
the technology folds back to component licensing. Pulled forward per the
red-team repair: the UL/IEC 62477-1 pre-submission conversation is filed in
2027 alongside the brassboard, not after it [L02-112].

## 9. 2030-2034 timing

Preparation 2026-2029: brassboard, certification filing, one paid embed
pilot with a shelf vendor, and an OCP contribution positioning the shelf
interface. Launch 2030: the Kyber generation (H2 2027) and Feynman (2028)
[P3R2-C-01-S06] will by then have installed two generations of 800 VDC
racks — early enough that the fleet is young, late enough that the
protection pain is documented in operations rather than PowerPoint.
Adoption 2030-2034: embed agreements with two-plus shelf vendors; CN
certified variant as the Chinese 800 V standard lands [P3R2-C-01-S03];
extension to 1500 V sidecar and MVDC campus feeders. Window risk runs both
directions: the socket may be contested early (scenario B), but the CN
standard vacuum and the multi-generation ramp keep secondary windows open
through the 15th Five-Year-Plan horizon.

## 10. Independent US and China paths

US path: UL/IEC 62477-1 certification [L02-112], OCP participation
[L02-044], and embed sales to the named shelf vendors [L02-043, L02-037].
The US leg's evidence is the platform owner's own gap statement — not
inference from China's. China path (independently evidenced): the OAII
forum's documented protection-standard vacuum [P3R2-C-01-S03], the
operating Alibaba-lineage HVDC estate [L02-048, L02-049], and the domestic
supply chain (Innoscience GaN [L02-050, P3R2-C-01-S01, R10-021], the
31-company chain [R10-019]) supporting a licensed CN entity that
manufactures with domestic devices and participates in the coming
standardization. COMPLIANCE GATE: China's gallium/germanium export
licensing regime (in force since August 2023, a licensing gate rather than
a ban) touches the CN variant's device supply and any cross-border GaN
movement [P3R2-C-01-S02]; the protection electronics themselves are
conventional EAR99-class instrumentation. The two legs share physics and
almost nothing else: separate certification regimes, separate supply
chains, separate buyer coalitions.

## 11. Side markets

Taiwan: Delta's MGX-ecosystem power racks make Taiwanese ODM co-design the
natural manufacturing and design-in channel [L02-037]. Japan: ROHM's SiC
adoption in AI-server BBUs marks the device-partnership channel [L02-051].
South Korea: LS Electric's presence in the funded competitive set
[P3R2-C-01-S04] makes Korea as much a competitor source as a market.
All optional; neither substitutes for the US/CN legs.

## 12. Regulation, safety, export controls

The governing safety standard is IEC 62477-1:2022 (power electronic
converter systems to 1500 VDC) [L02-112]; the US path adds UL listing on
the same technical base. China has no 800 V safety-certification standard
yet — the OAII forum says so explicitly [P3R2-C-01-S03] — which is
simultaneously the CN opportunity (first certified vendor) and the CN risk
(standard could be written around domestic incumbents; scenario D).
Export controls: EAR99-class electronics on the US side; China's Ga/Ge
licensing regime on the CN device supply [P3R2-C-01-S02]. No other control
trigger was identified in any session.

## 13. Capital and business model

v1 capital $8-20M — heavy, and honestly the portfolio's least
capital-efficient top-three idea, because certification campaigns and
power-lab infrastructure are real costs. The $350k decisive experiment
buys the kill decision cheaply, and the embed/licensing model (certified
shelf + reference-design licenses + per-fleet telemetry subscriptions
[L02-010]) shifts later capital onto shelf-vendor partners. The business
compounds through the certification asset: each additional vendor embed
amortizes the same certificate.

## 14. Founder skill transfer

Direct fit: microsecond fault-clearing circuits, arc-signature
discrimination (signal processing and learned detectors [L08-019]),
precharge/IMD sequencing, and fleet telemetry are instrumentation and
power-electronics engineering, the founder's core stack. The gap is
certification-process experience — hireable, and explicitly budgeted in
the v1 range.

## 15. Failure modes

(1) Spec absorption: a reference protection design ships integrated by
Delta/Vertiv-class vendors before the certification path completes — the
commercial kill test; mitigated by filing certification early and selling
embed economics, not hardware. (2) Better-capitalized incumbents: ABB
extends SACE Infinitus into the datacenter socket or DG Matrix's MGX
position converts to protection — mitigated per scenario B. (3)
Certification timeline overrun on a novel hybrid-breaker shelf — mitigated
by the 2027 pre-submission pull-forward. (4) Hyperscaler vertical
integration [L02-044] — mitigated per scenario C. (5) CN standard written
around domestic vendors — mitigated per scenario D via the licensed CN
entity.

## 15a. Residual risk assessment

The red-team decision was survive at medium residual risk with high
confidence, and the strongest counterargument deserves restating: the
reference-architecture owner is already seeding merchant power blocks
inside its ecosystem, and the protection layer may be claimed by
better-capitalized players before this venture's brassboard exists. The
honest response is that the thesis is bought for $350k and killed cheaply
if the socket closes; that the CN leg is structurally open to neither ABB
nor DG Matrix today; and that the quarterly re-verification cadence on DG
Matrix and ABB status (a standing stage-30 repair) converts the biggest
unknown into a tracked variable rather than a surprise. Both dominant
uncertainties are commercial, resolve on public evidence during 2027-2028,
and precede launch capital.
