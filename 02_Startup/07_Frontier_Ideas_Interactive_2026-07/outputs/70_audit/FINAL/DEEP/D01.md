# D01 — P3R2-D-02: Reel-to-reel contactless REBCO tape quality metrology

## 1. Thesis

The superconducting-tape industry is scaling from laboratory quantities to
industrial volume on a schedule that is now contract-dated rather than
forecast, and its quality-control layer has not scaled with it. FACT: Shanghai
Superconductor signed an exclusive RMB 1.6 billion long-term supply contract
for roughly 3,000 km of REBCO tape for the CAS/CNNC HL-4 tokamak, with
2025-2027 delivery, and has published a capacity trajectory from 4,000 km/yr
(2025) through an additional 6,000 km/yr under construction (2027-28) toward a
20,000 km/yr target by 2030 [P3R2-D-02-S02]. FACT: Furukawa/SuperPower
contracted several hundred kilometers of HTS wire to Tokamak Energy for
ST80-HTS, with scale-up under evaluation for the ST-E1 pilot plant [L03-044].
FACT: a 2025 peer-reviewed industry review counts roughly 15 manufacturers
worldwide with annual capacity above 5,000 km at 12 mm width, with four
top-tier PLD-route producers supplying over half of global output [R10-036].
INFERENCE: as volume triples-to-10x into the early 2030s, incoming-goods
acceptance becomes the binding constraint for magnet builders, because
fusion-grade coils concentrate an entire year of tape output into single
systems and a single undetected defect can destroy a multi-megajoule magnet
[L03-004, L03-018]. HYPOTHESIS: a neutral, merchant acceptance-metrology
vendor — selling calibrated, position-resolved critical-current and
delamination maps in a shared data format trusted by both vendor and buyer —
becomes the KLA of superconductors precisely because today's only commercial
instrument is sold by a tape producer and measures only one of the two
failure axes.

## 2. Customer job and named buyers

The customer job is incoming-goods acceptance and process-control feedback:
verify, at line speed and without contact, that every meter of purchased tape
meets its specified critical current, and screen for the mechanical defects
(delamination, dropouts, strain damage) that transport measurement on cut
samples cannot see. Named buyers on the magnet-builder side: Commonwealth
Fusion Systems (Devens tape/magnet factory; merchant magnet sales to Realta
Fusion and the University of Wisconsin WHAM experiment) [L03-035, R10-035];
Tokamak Energy/TE Magnetics with its Furukawa/SuperPower supply chain
[L03-044]; US ITER/ORNL magnet operations as a lab-side buyer [L03-030].
Named buyers on the producer side: Shanghai Superconductor (the HL-4
contractor) and Western Superconducting Technologies, whose ITER-strand QA
lineage (roughly 65% of ITER's NbTi strand) establishes the acceptance-data
culture this product formalizes [P3R2-D-02-S02, L03-031]. Grid operators form
a third buyer class: State Grid Shanghai has operated a kilometer-class
commercial HTS cable since 2021 and follow-on city deployments carry the same
conductor-QA burden [L03-041].

## 3. Product boundary and architecture

The product is a 77 K reel-to-reel acceptance scanner plus a reporting
standard, not a lab instrument and not a testing service alone. Architecture:
a dense Hall-sensor array (position-resolved remanent-field/critical-current
mapping) combined with lock-in thermal imaging for buried delamination and
dropout screening, at meters-per-second line speed, with transport-calibrated
Ic reporting against witness samples and a signed per-meter "conductor
passport" data format shared by vendor and buyer. Extensions bound into the
same platform: cable/CICC acceptance stations at the 10 kA class and coil
incoming-QA benches. Explicitly outside the boundary: tape manufacturing,
deposition process tooling, and magnet design services. The moat is the
neutral-referee position plus the calibrated correlation dataset linking
non-contact maps to destructive transport measurements — an asset that
compounds with every acceptance campaign.

## 4. Feasibility

Every element is demonstrated physics. Hall-array mapping of REBCO tape at
line speed is commercially practiced [NP45-001]; lock-in thermography is an
established NDT method; the engineering integration is line-speed data
acquisition, autonomous cryogen management, and calibration discipline —
instrumentation work, not new science. FACT: the incumbent instrument's own
datasheet specifies 21 Hall sensors across 12 mm, 1 mm axial resolution at
200 m/h measurement speed, 0-1 T applied field, a 50-1000 A measurement range
and ±3% accuracy — and lists no delamination or thermal channel of any kind
[NP45-001]. The peer-reviewed literature on critical-current scaling under
field and strain [L03-020, L03-021] and on quench/defect physics [L03-004,
L03-018] defines the acceptance criteria the instrument must resolve.
Remaining feasibility risk is calibration fidelity: proving that non-contact
maps predict transport Ic within 5% across producers' differing architectures
(the decisive experiment below).

Three engineering sub-problems deserve honest treatment. First, sensor
density and speed: exceeding the incumbent's 21-sensor/1 mm/200 m/h envelope
[NP45-001] toward meters-per-second requires higher-bandwidth Hall arrays and
synchronized acquisition — a scaling exercise in electronics, with the
physics limit set by field-relaxation timescales, not sensor speed. Second,
the thermal channel: lock-in thermography on a moving cryogenic tape demands
careful excitation design (inductive or optical heating pulses) and
frame-synchronized detection; the literature on strain-driven degradation
[L03-021] indicates the defect classes it must catch are millimeter-scale
and thermally distinct, which is favorable. Third, calibration transfer:
REBCO architectures differ across the roughly 15 producers [R10-036] in
stabilizer, substrate, and pinning; the correlation model must be qualified
per architecture family, which is exactly why the decisive experiment
requires blind correlation on at least two producers' tape rather than one.

## 5. Measurable 10x edge

Contactless, position-resolved Ic mapping at highway speed versus cut-sample
transport testing is an orders-of-magnitude jump in inspected fraction: from
sampling perhaps one meter in a thousand destructively to inspecting every
millimeter of every kilometer non-destructively. Against the incumbent
TapeStar class, the additive edge is the second failure axis — delamination
and buried-defect screening — which the incumbent's documented specification
does not offer [NP45-001], plus buyer-side trust: the incumbent is itself a
tape producer selling to its competitors, while this instrument's vendor
competes with no customer.

## 6. Demand

Durable demand evidence: the named contracts and buildouts above
[P3R2-D-02-S02, L03-044, L03-035]; the DOE Milestone-Based Fusion Development
Program with eight named awardee companies [L03-032]; US ITER's completed
central-solenoid deliveries evidencing the national-lab procurement channel
[L03-030]; ASIPP's 35.1 T record magnet program as the CN institutional
demand pole [L03-029, L03-037]. Refresh-sensitive demand evidence: the
2025-2026 capacity announcements and the CFS-Realta partnership (April 2026,
potentially multi-billion-dollar) [R10-035, R10-036] — these date the volume
ramp but must be re-verified annually. The volume ramp is peer-reviewed as
well as contract-dated: the largest-volume HTS wire order to date was
delivered to a commercial fusion customer within nine months at record
engineering current density [L03-003], and the tape industry's own
literature frames fusion demand as the trigger forcing PLD-route
manufacturing scale-up — with exactly the quality-control challenges this
product addresses [L03-019]. Demand scales with conductor volume,
not with fusion success: even if no tokamak ever reaches breakeven, every
kilometer of tape sold into cables, research magnets, industrial field
coils, and naval systems — AMSC's HTS degaussing units are designed into
the San Antonio-class ship platform with deliveries since 2022 [L03-046]
— passes through acceptance. That low-beta property is the thesis's most
attractive feature.

Bottom-up arithmetic (every input labeled). FACT: Shanghai Superconductor
alone plans 20,000 km/yr by 2030 and is described as one of only two
producers above 1,000 km/yr at the time of its disclosure [P3R2-D-02-S02].
ASSUMPTION: an acceptance scanner installation costs $0.5-2M per line
(precision-metrology capital-equipment analogy; no direct price source
exists — an explicitly open gap). ASSUMPTION: each of the roughly 15
manufacturers above 5,000 km/yr [R10-036] needs one to three lines to cover
capacity, and each major magnet builder needs one incoming-goods station.
INFERENCE: producer-side hardware alone is a $10-50M cumulative opportunity
by 2030, before buyer-side stations, cable-project campaigns, and recurring
acceptance services. This is deliberately conservative decision arithmetic,
not a market-size claim; the services and data-licensing layers are where a
metrology franchise compounds, and none of the totals depends on any single
fusion program succeeding.

A second demand layer is regulatory-grade traceability. Fusion pilot plants
will be licensed facilities; their magnet systems will need documented
conductor provenance the way aerospace fasteners need mill certificates.
INFERENCE: the first vendor whose acceptance format is written into a
procurement specification converts a metrology product into a compliance
standard — the highest-margin position in any instrument category. The
2028-2029 milestones below are sequenced to reach that position before the
volume ramp peaks.

## 7. Competition

FACT: THEVA's TapeStar is a real merchant instrument — sold openly through
distributors and used by many laboratories and well-known tape producers —
correcting any assumption that only captive in-house scanners exist. Its
documented capability is Ic mapping only [NP45-001]. Lake Shore-class
component vendors supply Hall probes and cryogenic sensors but no integrated
reel-to-reel acceptance system. In-house scanners exist at leading producers.
No independent, merchant, buyer-trusted acceptance vendor selling across
producers and magnet builders was identified in any 2026 verification pass
[P3R2-D-02-S01]. Competitive risks: THEVA adding a delamination channel;
magnet builders standardizing on comparing producers' own TapeStar maps
(accepting vendor self-QC); an exclusive instrument-producer partnership
foreclosing the neutral seat. The counter to each is speed to the calibrated
correlation dataset and early standardization of the acceptance format.

### 7a. Competitive scenarios and response plan

Scenario A (most likely): THEVA continues selling TapeStar as an Ic-mapping
line while producers keep in-house tooling; the neutral-acceptance seat stays
open through 2030. Response: standard plan. Scenario B: THEVA launches a
delamination-capable TapeStar within two years. Response: the differentiation
shifts entirely to neutrality and the cross-producer correlation dataset —
which a producer-owned vendor structurally cannot offer to its competitors —
plus the buyer-side stations THEVA does not serve. Scenario C: a magnet
builder (CFS-class) builds internal acceptance at factory scale and
open-sources its method. Response: pivot the product center of gravity to
producer-side lines and cable-project campaigns, where an internal
magnet-factory method does not reach. Scenario D: a Chinese institute
publishes a free reference scanner design. Response: compete on calibration
traceability and certified reporting rather than hardware — the signed,
witness-sample-calibrated passport is the product, and certification
authority does not commoditize with a schematic. In all four scenarios the
$120k experiment and early format standardization remain the correct first
moves, which is why the plan is robust to competitive ordering.

## 8. Decisive 2026-2029 experiment

2027 benchtop campaign: build a 77 K reel-to-reel Hall-array scanner segment;
scan 100 m of commercial tape from at least two producers; blind-correlate
predicted versus destructive transport Ic on 50 cut samples. Budget:
$120,000 (the cheapest decisive experiment in the portfolio). Thresholds:
prediction error under 5% on at least 90% of samples; demonstrated detection
of at least one seeded delamination/dropout class per producer architecture
that Ic mapping alone misses. KILL RULE: if blind correlation cannot reach 5%
across producer architectures, or if seeded mechanical defects cannot be
discriminated from Ic noise, the neutral-acceptance thesis fails and the
venture folds to a calibration-services niche. Follow-on 2028: a paid
acceptance pilot with one magnet builder; 2029: the cable/CICC station
prototype and publication of the conductor-passport format.

## 9. 2030-2034 timing

Preparation 2026-2029: experiment, pilot, format publication, and the
correlation dataset across producers — none requiring an operating company
beyond a small instrumentation team. Launch 2030: coincides with the
contract-dated capacity ramp (Shanghai Superconductor's 20,000 km/yr target
year) and the CFS ARC-class and Tokamak Energy ST-E1 pilot-plant magnet
procurement windows [P3R2-D-02-S02, L03-044, R10-035]. Adoption 2030-2034:
acceptance stations at two-plus producers and two-plus magnet builders;
cable-project acceptance campaigns as HTS grid deployments spread [L03-041];
the passport format referenced in at least one procurement specification.
Window-risk: if fusion capital contracts sharply, tape volume growth slows —
but the 2025-2027 contracts already signed carry the early market
regardless.

## 10. Independent US and China paths

US path: sell scanners and acceptance campaigns to CFS-ecosystem magnet
builders and national labs; anchor credibility via a lab co-calibration
(NIST-traceable transport benches); the DOE Milestone cohort provides eight
named prospects [L03-032, L03-035]. China path (independent evidence, not a
mirror): Shanghai Superconductor's contracted volume and stated capacity
target create producer-side acceptance demand documented in its own
disclosures [P3R2-D-02-S02], inside an eighteen-year Shanghai HTS industry
chain with three named tape producers spanning both PLD and MOD deposition
routes [L03-052]; State Grid's operating cable and ASIPP's magnet
program provide buyer-side acceptance poles [L03-041, L03-029], with the
CFETR toroidal-field program — 14.5 T peak field, prototype coil
manufacture at ASIPP under the CRAFT facility — as the institutional
magnet-scale demand marker [L03-024]. Market entry
via instrument sales/licensed local service is the standard, open route for
test equipment. COMPLIANCE GATE: Western Superconducting Technologies and
ASIPP screening returned inconclusive negatives against aggregated
export-control datasets — a conclusive BIS Consolidated Screening List
resolution is required before any CN transaction [P3R2-D-02-S02 context];
the instrument class itself carries no identified control trigger.

## 11. Side markets

Japan: Furukawa/SuperPower's supply chain makes a JP-based producer
relationship natural [L03-044]. South Korea: the KEPCO/LS superconducting

## 12. Regulation, safety, export controls

No dedicated certification regime governs tape acceptance instruments; the
relevant standards family is IEC 61788 (superconductor critical-current test
methods), whose text remains unfetched across all sessions — an explicit
open item carried to the audit [pack gap]. Liquid-nitrogen handling is
ordinary lab safety. Export controls: measurement instruments of this class
are not named on control lists; the counterparty-screening gate above is the
operative compliance task.

## 13. Capital and business model

v1 capital $1.5-4M — the most capital-efficient idea in the final 24.
Business model: instrument sales (scanners, stations) plus per-campaign
acceptance services plus format/data licensing; consumables-light. The
services leg smooths the lumpy instrument cycle and feeds the correlation
dataset. Path to sellable v1 well under $5M cumulative is credible because
the decisive experiment is $120k and the beachhead product is a bench-scale
station.

## 14. Founder skill transfer

Direct fit: Hall-array magnetic sensing, lock-in thermal imaging, line-speed
data acquisition, and calibrated reporting are the founder's core
instrumentation, electromagnetics, and numerical-analysis skills. The
cryogenic mechanics are packaged, catalog-grade engineering — pulse-tube
coolers of the PT205 class are ordered from a datasheet [L03-050]. Among the ten
deep dives, this is the strongest founder-to-product mapping.

## 15. Failure modes

(1) Vendor self-QC acceptance: magnet builders standardize on producers'
TapeStar data — mitigated by targeting buyer-side procurement specifications
early. (2) Incumbent response: THEVA adds delamination — mitigated by the
correlation dataset and neutrality, which a producer-owned instrument cannot
replicate. (3) Calibration transfer failure across producer architectures —
the kill rule catches this at $120k. (4) Volume disappointment: fusion
capital winter slows the ramp — the cable/grid and research-magnet legs
carry a smaller but real business. (5) CN compliance surprise on WST/ASIPP —
resolved before any CN commitment.

## 15a. Residual risk assessment

The red-team decision for this idea was survive at medium residual risk with
high confidence, and nothing in pack construction changed that. The two
uncertainties that most deserve continued tracking are commercial rather
than technical: whether buyer-side procurement culture in fusion matures fast
enough to pay for neutral acceptance before 2030 (the WHAM- and
Realta-class merchant magnet sales suggest yes, because arms-length
transactions force arms-length quality evidence [L03-035, R10-035]), and
whether the CN compliance gate resolves cleanly enough to keep the China leg
open (if it does not, the US/JP/KR legs still clear the venture's modest
capital bar). Both resolve with facts, not opinions, during 2027-2028 — well
before launch capital is committed.
