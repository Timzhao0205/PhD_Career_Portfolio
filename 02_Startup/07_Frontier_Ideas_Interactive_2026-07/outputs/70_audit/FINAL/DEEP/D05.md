# D05 — P3R2-E-14: Multi-terminal DC protection relay and vendor-neutral HIL qualification

## 1. Thesis

The United States is finally building long-distance DC transmission, and
the protection layer for anything more meshed than point-to-point does
not commercially exist as a neutral product. FACT: three independent
developers hold committed capital — Grain Belt Express with nearly $1.7
billion in contractor awards to Quanta and Kiewit for an 800-mile,
5,000 MW multi-terminal line spanning four grid regions, construction
targeted from 2026 [P3R2-E-14-S01]; Pattern Energy's Southern Spirit, a
320-mile, ±525 kV, ~3,000 MW VSC-HVDC interconnector (~$2.6 billion
private, up to $360 million DOE support) now in permitting with
construction targeted ~2029 and in-service ~2032 [L08-041, R10-053,
R10-054]; and Grid United's four-project pipeline led by the North
Plains Connector [P3R2-E-14-S02]. FACT: FERC Order 1920 mandates
long-term regional transmission planning with interregional coordination
[P3R2-E-14-S03], and FERC has simultaneously tightened protection and
ride-through standards for inverter-based resources (PRC-029-1)
[L08-043]. FACT: fault discrimination in multi-terminal DC grids is a
solved research problem — traveling-wave, wavelet, and coordination
algorithms are published with hardware-in-the-loop validation [L08-005,
L08-004, L08-007, L08-006] — but no vendor-neutral relay or
qualification authority sells that capability. INFERENCE: when a
construction wave meets a missing safety layer, the buyer's first
purchase is proof — benchmarking, qualification, acceptance testing —
and the second is hardware. HYPOTHESIS: a venture that first builds the
vendor-neutral HIL qualification platform for DC protection, then ships
the sub-millisecond discrimination relay it qualifies, becomes the SEL
of DC grids — the trusted third party of the DC century.

## 2. Customer job and named buyers

The customer job splits in two. Job one (2027-2029): qualification —
prove, on a hardware-in-the-loop bench against realistic four-terminal
models, that a protection scheme discriminates internal faults from
external disturbances fast enough to save the converters, before a
developer commits financing-grade guarantees. Job two (2030+): the relay
itself — sub-millisecond trip decisions in the station. Named buyers:
the three developer organizations above (Pattern, Invenergy/GBX, Grid
United) [L08-041, P3R2-E-14-S01, P3R2-E-14-S02]; the AC-side incumbents'
customers — utilities and ISOs navigating FERC 1920 interregional
studies [P3R2-E-14-S03] and PRC-029 compliance [L08-043]; GE
Vernova-scale integrators whose ~$10 billion HVDC backlog and
electrification order book quantify the equipment wave the protection
layer must serve [L08-033]. Korean side-market buyers: KEPCO's West
Coast 'Energy Expressway' — four HVDC lines, eight converter stations
sited, first phase accelerated to 2030, ~KRW 11.5 trillion total with
~KRW 4.8 trillion converter-related spend — with LS Electric, Hyosung,
HD Hyundai and Iljin named as localization candidates [L08-038, L08-039,
P3R2-E-14-S05]. China is a license-only channel: NR Electric already
sells LCC/VSC control-and-protection systems and a DC breaker
domestically [P3R2-E-14-S04], and XJ Electric's RMB 1.275 billion State
Grid tender win shows a closed, domestic procurement world [L08-034] —
addressed by licensing test suites, never by selling relays.

## 3. Product boundary and architecture

Phase one product: a vendor-neutral qualification platform — real-time
digital simulator models of multi-terminal VSC networks, a published
benchmark protocol built on the peer-reviewed algorithm base [L08-005,
L08-007], fault libraries including high-impedance and pole-to-ground
cases [L08-004], and acceptance reporting developers can put in front of
lenders. Phase two product: a DC protection relay — traveling-wave and
wavelet discrimination in deterministic firmware, sub-millisecond
decision latency, coordinated tripping against hybrid breakers of the
class Hitachi Energy markets [L08-052] and the research literature
refines [L08-001, L08-003], plus arc-detection channels adapted from the
photovoltaic DC literature [L08-019, L08-020, L08-021] for station DC
auxiliary systems. Explicitly outside the boundary: converters, DC
breakers themselves, station engineering, and AC protection. The moat is
benchmark authority plus the qualified-algorithm dataset — the same
neutral-referee economics as the portfolio's metrology ideas, applied to
grid safety.

## 4. Feasibility

The discrimination physics is published and validated: adaptive
non-unit traveling-wave protection for multi-terminal lines [L08-005],
wavelet schemes validated on four-terminal models [L08-004], systematic
wavelet-selection studies with HIL validation [L08-007], and
coordination frameworks for half-bridge MMC grids [L08-006]. SSCB and
breaker-coordination literature defines the actuator side [L08-017,
L08-001, L08-003]. Three engineering sub-problems deserve honest
treatment. First, model fidelity: qualification is only bankable if the
RTDS models reproduce converter control behavior faithfully enough that
vendors accept adverse results — the platform must be co-validated
against at least one vendor's control replicas early. Second,
deterministic latency: a sub-millisecond trip decision through
measurement, discrimination, and coordination requires relay-grade
embedded engineering — the founder's stack — not simulation. Third,
acceptance economics: the benchmark must map onto the compliance
machinery utilities already know (PRC-029-class filings [L08-043]) so
that qualification reports slot into existing regulatory processes
rather than inventing new ones.

## 5. Measurable 10x edge

Against the status quo — protection bundled opaquely inside each
converter vendor's station package [L08-052] — the qualification
platform offers something that does not exist at any price: an
independent, reproducible, lender-grade answer to "will this grid
survive a fault?" The relay's edge is benchable: sub-millisecond
discrimination on four-terminal topologies versus the multi-millisecond
AC-inherited practice, with published false-trip and missed-fault rates
under high-impedance conditions [L08-005]. The 10x is in decision
speed and in trust: the only protection numbers a developer can show a
counterparty that were not produced by the vendor being evaluated.

## 6. Demand

Durable demand evidence: the three-developer pipeline with committed
capital and contractor awards [P3R2-E-14-S01, L08-041, P3R2-E-14-S02];
DOE's anchor-customer support for Southern Spirit [R10-054]; FERC 1920's
structural mandate for interregional planning [P3R2-E-14-S03]; GE
Vernova's HVDC backlog and order growth [L08-033]. Refresh-sensitive
evidence: Southern Spirit's schedule (construction ~2029, in-service
~2032, resolved to the developer's own page after a cross-source
conflict) [R10-053]; GBX pre-construction status with Illinois Supreme
Court clearance (January 2026, stage-30 verification). Side-market
evidence: KEPCO's accelerated 2030 first phase and quantified
converter-station spend [L08-039, P3R2-E-14-S05, L08-038]. CN evidence
(license-only): the closed domestic tender world [L08-034] and NR
Electric's incumbent product lines [P3R2-E-14-S04].

Bottom-up arithmetic (every input labeled). FACT: KEPCO's program alone
carries ~KRW 4.8 trillion of converter-related spend [P3R2-E-14-S05];
the US pipeline sums to roughly $6-8 billion of committed line capital
[P3R2-E-14-S01, L08-041]. ASSUMPTION: protection and qualification
content runs 0.5-2% of converter-station capital (relay bays, test
campaigns, acceptance programs — pricing explicitly unsourced, a
red-team-flagged gap). INFERENCE: a $30-150M cumulative protection and
qualification opportunity across the named 2026-2032 pipeline, before
any meshed-grid expansion or MVDC datacenter adjacency [L08-017]. The
load-bearing conclusion is modest: two or three qualification campaigns
plus one relay design win fund the company through launch; the named
pipeline supports that without any speculative project.

The sensitivity that matters most in that arithmetic is not price but
timing: qualification revenue is front-loaded (it is bought during
design and financing, years before energization), while relay revenue is
back-loaded (bought at station integration). A one-year slip in every
named project therefore delays hardware income but barely moves the
2028-2029 services case, because FERC-1920-driven interregional studies
[P3R2-E-14-S03] and PRC-029 compliance work [L08-043] generate
qualification demand even from projects that never break ground. That
asymmetry is why the red-team repair resequenced the business onto the
platform first — it converts schedule risk, the pipeline's dominant
risk, into a hardware-timing question rather than a survival question.

## 7. Competition

The station incumbents — Hitachi Energy [L08-052], Siemens (whose new
DC protection portfolio is vendor-integrated, per stage-30
verification), GE Vernova [L08-033] — sell protection inside their own
packages; none offers neutral qualification of a rival's scheme. SEL,
the AC relay trust incumbent, lists no DC-specific relay beyond its
T401L line, with an unspecified "coming soon" (stage-30 verification) —
the most dangerous potential entrant, and the reason speed matters. NR
Electric and XJ own the closed Chinese market [P3R2-E-14-S04, L08-034].
The neutral relay-plus-qualification seat is empty in the West.

### 7a. Competitive scenarios and response plan

Scenario A (most likely): vendors keep bundling; developers and lenders
increasingly demand independent verification as multi-terminal risk
grows. Response: standard plan — qualification platform first (sellable
even to vendor-integrated projects, per the red-team repair), relay
second. Scenario B: SEL ships a true DC discrimination relay by 2028.
Response: the qualification platform becomes SEL's benchmark too —
authority migrates to whoever runs the bench, and the venture's relay
pivots to the MVDC/datacenter class [L08-017] where SEL's utility
franchise is weakest. Scenario C: converter vendors refuse third-party
protection in their stations entirely — the red team's strongest
counterargument. Response: sell qualification to the developer/lender
side of the table, which vendors cannot veto, and enter hardware through
station DC auxiliary and arc-protection subsystems [L08-019, L08-021]
that sit outside the converter warranty boundary. Scenario D: the US
pipeline slips wholesale into the 2030s. Response: the KEPCO program's
2030 first phase [L08-039] and CN test-suite licensing [P3R2-E-14-S04]
carry the qualification business; the relay waits — capital exposure
before 2029 is a $300k experiment plus platform engineering, not a
factory.

## 8. Decisive 2026-2029 experiment

2027 campaign: implement the three strongest published discrimination
algorithms [L08-005, L08-004, L08-007] on a real-time HIL bench against
a four-terminal VSC model; benchmark trip latency, false-trip and
missed-fault rates across fault libraries including high-impedance
cases; then build a relay prototype demonstrating sub-millisecond trip
decisions end-to-end. Budget: $300,000. Thresholds: at least one
algorithm family achieving <1 ms decisions with discrimination
performance matching its published claims; the prototype reproducing
bench performance in hardware. KILL RULE: if no published algorithm
discriminates reliably on the four-terminal model, or the <1 ms
end-to-end path cannot be met in relay-grade hardware, the relay thesis
dies and the venture folds to simulation services. Follow-on 2028: one
developer or ISO/RTO study engagement (the red-team repair's dated
milestone); 2029: first paid qualification campaign on a named project.

## 9. 2030-2034 timing

The construction wave lands protection procurement exactly in the
window: GBX construction from 2026 with energization phases into the
early 2030s [P3R2-E-14-S01]; Grid United projects from ~2028
[P3R2-E-14-S02]; Southern Spirit construction ~2029, in-service ~2032
[R10-053]; KEPCO first phase 2030 [L08-039]. Launch 2030 puts the relay
product exactly at the first acceptance-testing cycles, with the
qualification platform already earning from 2028-2029. The honest
timing risk runs the other way — US multi-terminal DC could stay
point-to-point through 2034 (the key kill), which is why phase-one
revenue does not depend on meshed topologies: even point-to-point
stations need qualified protection and PRC-029-adjacent compliance
evidence [L08-043].

## 10. Independent US and China paths

US path (primary): the developer pipeline, FERC-mandated planning, and
the qualification-first sales motion described above. China path: no
equity presence and no relay sales — the CN market is closed
procurement [L08-034] served by domestic incumbents [P3R2-E-14-S04];
the only CN motion is licensing HIL test suites and benchmark
protocols, which stage-20 export screening cleared (State Grid and XJ
are not Entity-Listed; EAR classification review flagged as a standing
gate). The portfolio's US/China balance does not lean on this idea; its
geography honesty is the point — a US-centric infrastructure play with
a bounded, license-only CN channel.

## 11. Side markets

South Korea is the substantive side market: KEPCO's quantified program,
accelerated schedule, and localization posture [L08-038, L08-039,
P3R2-E-14-S05] make a Korean qualification partnership (with an LS
Electric-class localizer) the natural first international campaign.
The Korean government's 2027 HVDC-localization target [L08-038] works
in the venture's favor: localizers need independent evidence that
domestically built converters and protection meet international
practice, which is precisely what a neutral qualification bench sells,
and which no Korean incumbent can self-certify credibly. Datacenter
MVDC distribution [L08-017] is the domestic adjacency where the same
discrimination firmware serves a faster-moving buyer class.

## 12. Regulation, safety, export controls

US: NERC/FERC compliance machinery (PRC-029-1 and successors) is the
regulatory frame qualification reports must map onto [L08-043]; relays
themselves certify under established IEC/IEEE relay standards, and the
absence of a DC-specific relay standard is itself part of the
opportunity — the venture's published benchmark is positioned to become
the reference practice the eventual standard codifies, the same path AC
relay standards took from de facto practice to codification. Export:
US base case clean; the CN license-only channel carries a standing EAR
classification review before any transaction; no Entity-List conflict
exists today (stage-20 screening). Korea: standard industrial export,
no control triggers identified.

## 13. Capital and business model

v1 capital $3-8M: HIL platform engineering, relay firmware, and two
qualification campaigns' working capital. Business model sequenced by
the red-team repair: qualification services and test-suite licensing
first (2028-2029 revenue, low capital), relay hardware second (2030+,
station-grade margins), CN/KR licensing third. The $300k experiment is
the largest single pre-company check and buys the entire technical
verdict; every later dollar is spent against a customer-funded
qualification campaign or a signed relay scope, never ahead of one.

## 14. Founder skill transfer

Direct fit: discrimination algorithms are signal processing; the relay
is deterministic embedded firmware; the platform is HIL/DAQ
engineering — all founder-native. Grid-industry credibility is the gap,
closed by the benchmark-publication strategy: in protection, authority
accrues to whoever runs the open bench, not to the oldest logo.

## 15. Failure modes

(1) US DC stays point-to-point through 2034 — the key kill; hedged by
qualification revenue that point-to-point stations still need, KEPCO,
and MVDC. (2) Vendor refusal to admit third-party protection (scenario
C). (3) SEL entry (scenario B). (4) Project-specific political
opposition — real (Louisiana SB108, Entergy's PSC filing
[P3R2-E-14-S06]) but pipeline-wide only if it generalizes, which three
independent developers across different states make unlikely. (5)
Algorithm non-discrimination — caught at $300k by the kill rule.

## 15a. Residual risk assessment

Red-team decision: survive, medium residual risk, high confidence. The
repair that reshaped this plan — qualification suite first, relay
second — converts the strongest counterargument (nobody unbundles
safety from the converter vendor) from a kill into a sequencing
constraint: the first product sells to the side of the table that wants
vendors checked, not to the vendors. The dominant uncertainties (GBX
construction actually starting; JBCS-style protection scopes appearing
in developer RFPs; SEL's DC posture) all resolve on public evidence by
2028, ahead of any hardware capital.
