# Baseline hypotheses for independent verification

These are preliminary triage hypotheses from a direct read of the supplied
manuscript, not final answers and not legal advice. The assigned Fable stages
must challenge them against current prior art.

## Likely answer to the user's central question

The **AlGaN/GaN Hall-effect sensor itself is unlikely to be newly patentable from
this publication merely because it was used as a fusion magnetic diagnostic**.
The manuscript uses an established AlGaN/GaN 2DEG Hall structure, a geometry and
high-temperature behavior already published by the group, and conventional Hall
bias/readout principles. Earlier fusion work also used Hall probes for direct,
steady-state, and edge-plasma magnetic diagnostics.

A genuinely new and non-obvious use of an old structure can sometimes support a
process claim, but a new result or newly recognized property alone does not make
the old device new. Here, the use case faces both sides of the prior-art
combination: GaN Hall sensing in harsh environments and Hall sensing in fusion.
Any surviving use claim would probably need concrete, non-routine deployment
constraints and an unexpected technical result, not only "place this known
sensor in a stellarator."

## Publication feature most worth OTL review

The strongest publication-specific candidate appears to be the exact **UHV and
glow-discharge-cleaning protected in-vessel module combination**:

- AlGaN/GaN Hall die on a ceramic LCC with aluminum wire bonds;
- EPO-TEK 353ND encapsulation and a 150 degrees C one-hour vacuum bake;
- zirconia holder and stainless standoff; and
- a grounded graphite shield over the module to reduce GDC arcing and epoxy
  degradation while retaining useful magnetic access.

This is still a thin candidate. Epoxy coating plus vacuum bake, ceramic support,
and a grounded protective shield may each be routine. Its case improves only if
the particular geometry/grounding/material arrangement solved a documented
failure mode, produced unexpected UHV/GDC performance, or enabled a useful
combination that close references did not teach. The manuscript provides no
dimensions, comparison without the shield, outgassing data, residual-gas data,
thermal result, or GDC exposure metric. Those gaps may make the disclosure too
basic for useful claims.

## Other manuscript features that appear weak alone

- The regular octagonal Hall plate and fabrication sequence.
- Off-the-shelf INA849/OPA814 differential amplification at 1 MHz.
- Biased/unbiased and plasma/coil-only controls.
- Temporal comparison with the diamagnetic loop.
- Operation across 68 shots or a claim of first GaN Hall deployment in a
  stellarator, without a new claimable structure or method.

Scientific importance, first demonstration, and patentability are different
tests. A valuable paper can contain no commercially useful patent claim.

## Preliminary publication action

Because Stanford OTL expressly treats preprints as public disclosures and many
foreign systems lack the U.S. grace period, the conservative action is to ask
OTL for a rapid confidential review **before arXiv** if the advisor believes the
graphite-shielded module contains a protectable contribution. The review can be
short and candid: identify the one plausible combination, the conventional
pieces, the evidence gaps, possible UW-Madison co-inventorship, and sponsorship.

The final Fable stage may instead conclude that no publication-specific filing
case is commercially meaningful. Even then, OTL/PI clearance is the right place
to resolve university and sponsor obligations; the package does not make that
decision for them.
