## Decision: `NO_PUBLICATION_SPECIFIC_FILING_CASE_IDENTIFIED`

No element of this publication supports a useful patent position on the public
record. The device is the group's own published 2019/2020 prior art; the fusion
use exploits known, group-published properties of a known device in a field
with in-vessel semiconductor Hall deployments since 2005; the readout is
catalog electronics; the validation is standard commissioning practice; and
the one surviving candidate — the grounded graphite shield of the UHV/GDC
module — is a predictable assembly of documented fusion-engineering practices
(GDC arcing and grounding physics documented since 1979) disclosed publicly in
exactly one unquantified sentence, with no unexpected result and no enabling
detail. A narrow contingent case exists only if inventor-held evidence
(G1+G2+G3: a documented no-shield failure, conception records, drawings)
materializes; none has been produced.

**This label does not by itself clear posting.** A short, time-boxed
pre-posting checkpoint remains recommended — because an arXiv post is permanent
and license-irrevocable, because shield conception (and therefore ownership)
may sit partly with UW-Madison co-authors or HSX facility staff, and because
Bayh-Dole sponsor reporting applies whether or not anything is patentable. The
checkpoint protects ownership hygiene and sponsor duties, not the shield's
filing prospects.

## The three questions, one line each

1. **Can the Hall sensor itself be patented now?** No — it is the group's own
   published prior art (geometry, anneal recipe, 576 °C data) plus purchased
   and routine inputs; nothing new is disclosed. (Brief §2.1.)
2. **Does stellarator/fusion use support a meaningful new-use case?** No — the
   use exploits known published properties (the *In re May* problem) and reads
   as a standard KSR material substitution over 2005–2014 InSb/GaAs in-vessel
   deployments; the genuine scientific first is best protected by publishing
   promptly, not by filing. (Brief §2.2.)
3. **Is the UHV/GDC module worth OTL review or too routine/thin?** Too
   routine/thin to present as a filing candidate — six of seven elements are
   vendor-standard, and the shield fails the best adversarial case — but
   include it, candidly framed, in the checkpoint intake anyway: sponsor
   reporting and the ownership questions do not depend on patentability.
   (Brief §2.3.)

## Checkpoint mechanics (Brief §4–§6)

- **Within 48 h:** PI sets a decision date (one to two weeks out, PI's
  choice); circulate the G-question list (who conceived the shield; was it

  anywhere; did GDC actually run; any no-shield failure records); send the

  response timeline; establish IEEE review status; start the arXiv source
  scrub (strip `.aux`/`.log`/`.synctex.gz`/built PDF; never upload the cover
  letter).
- **At the decision date, default release:** a documented, informed PI/author
  decision to post without a filing (release condition (b)). The modal outcome
  is a documented "no filing — clear to post," obtained in days.
- **Early exits:** OTL clears or files a provisional (condition (a)), or an
  earlier public disclosure of the shield is confirmed (condition (c)). A
  provisional is rational only if the G1+G2+G3 bundle surfaces inside the
  window; full prosecution is not justified on any evidenced scenario.
