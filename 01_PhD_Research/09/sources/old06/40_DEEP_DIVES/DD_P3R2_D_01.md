# P3R2-D-01 — Merchant HTS-magnet quench detection and protection

**Frozen portfolio position:** rank 3; score 62.8/100; confidence low; scientific/big-physics system; US beachhead; China not countable; TRL 3; $250,000 first full experiment. All gates remain as selected: G1, G4, G5, G6, and G7 pass marginal; G2 and G3 pass.

## Investment thesis

The investable wedge is not “quench protection for every superconductor.” It is a jointly qualified safety subsystem for new, high-energy REBCO magnets whose builders cannot rely on conventional voltage-tap logic alone. REBCO can tolerate a disturbance without immediately forming a large normal zone, yet a small normal zone can remain spatially concentrated and create a destructive hot spot before a clear terminal-voltage signal emerges. The product combines co-wound distributed optical sensing, RF/acoustic and electrical channels, deterministic trigger logic, an interface to active energy extraction, and a qualification dossier tied to a specific coil architecture. The scientific need is durable: current literature continues to identify HTS quench detection and protection as a distinct engineering problem ([L03-004](https://doi.org/10.3390/instruments5030027)), while REBCO conductor performance remains strongly coupled to strain, delamination, and electromagnetic-mechanical stress ([L03-018](https://academic.oup.com/nsr/article/10/3/nwad001/6973212), [L03-020](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6668/ad7f95), [L03-021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0920379623001072)).

The commercial case is narrower. Federal awards, national-lab magnet deliveries, private fusion construction, and external magnet transactions prove that identifiable US organizations spend on superconducting magnets. They do not prove that those organizations will buy an independent protection package. The company therefore begins as a partner-led qualification program, not a catalog-instrument vendor. Its asset is a defensible body of precursor signatures, false-trigger data, coil-specific models, and acceptance procedures accumulated across controlled campaigns. If that dataset lets a buyer shorten qualification or substantiate a magnet warranty, it can become more valuable than the sensing hardware itself.

This remains a low-confidence idea because the closest buyers are also formidable internal competitors, representative-magnet access is indispensable, and a safety failure can destroy a unique asset. The thesis is attractive only if one US magnet builder allows progressive testing and eventually treats the subsystem as a separable, auditable safety layer. There is no claimed signed access, purchase order, or paid evaluation today.

## Exact buyers, exact job, and current demand proof

The first buyer is the chief magnet engineer or protection lead at a US fusion company building REBCO coils. Commonwealth Fusion Systems is the clearest named example because it is installing a multi-magnet SPARC system and has publicly connected that build to an early-2030s ARC ambition ([P3R2-D-01-S01](https://www.prismnews.com/hobbies/nuclear-reactions/commonwealth-fusion-systems-installs-first-sparc-magnet-eyes-2026-plasma-milestone)). CFS has also sold magnets to Realta Fusion, previously supplied the University of Wisconsin WHAM experiment, and licensed technology to Type One Fusion, evidence that magnet technology can cross organizational boundaries rather than remaining entirely captive ([L03-035](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/02/commonwealth-fusion-systems-leans-on-magnets-for-near-term-revenue/)). Tokamak Energy/TE Magnetics is another buyer archetype and a direct build-versus-buy threat: it is capitalized to commercialize magnet technology and has established large-scale conductor supply relationships ([L03-033](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tokamak-energy-raises-125m-to-commercialize-transformative-fusion-and-magnet-technologies-302310607.html), [L03-044](https://www.furukawaelectric.com/en/release/2023/kenkai_20230112.html)).

The second buyer class is a US national laboratory or university high-field-magnet program that needs an instrumented safety package for a one-off research coil, test stand, or facility magnet. US ITER’s completed central-solenoid procurement package confirms that complex superconducting-magnet delivery and integration remain active national-lab work ([L03-030](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/us-iter-completes-central-solenoid-procurement-package)). The Department of Energy’s Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program names eight private fusion awardees, including CFS and Tokamak Energy, and supplies an official funding channel for engineering milestones ([L03-032](https://www.energy.gov/articles/us-department-energy-announces-selectees-107-million-fusion-innovation-research-engine)).

The job is precise: detect a localized thermal, strain, or electromagnetic precursor early enough to initiate a safe response; avoid nuisance trips during current ramps, cryogenic transients, and mechanical events; and produce traceable evidence that the protection behavior is repeatable. The buyer is not purchasing “fusion exposure.” It is purchasing reduced probability of losing an expensive coil, faster root-cause analysis after an anomaly, and a defensible qualification record.

Demand proof is only marginal under the portfolio’s literal gate. The sources demonstrate magnet construction, awards, and external magnet transactions, but no source in the accepted pack is a tender or order for a merchant quench subsystem. That gap governs the commercialization plan: discovery interviews and partner qualification must precede product scaling. A host program’s spending cannot be silently converted into subsystem revenue.

## Product boundary

The design-in product has five elements: a co-wound optical sensing path; conventional voltage taps plus selected RF/acoustic channels; isolated acquisition and deterministic FPGA sensor fusion; a protection-initiation output connected to the magnet’s approved dump or quench-spreading hardware; and a qualification dossier containing calibration, precursor-event, nuisance-trigger, and failure-injection data. A service layer maintains calibration, event review, and configuration control.

The company does **not** sell the REBCO conductor, winding, cryostat, main magnet supply, cryogenic plant, structural case, or complete machine safety case. It does not promise to discharge a high-energy magnet in less than a millisecond. The credible claim is sub-millisecond decision and initiation after a validated threshold; the full current decay is constrained by inductance, allowed terminal voltage, insulation, dump resistance, and coil-specific current-sharing behavior. It also cannot retrofit co-wound fiber into a finished winding. An existing coil could receive upgraded acquisition, surface sensing, or dump controls, but the full distributed-sensing proposition must be designed in before winding.

This boundary makes the qualification dossier essential. The same electronics cannot be dropped unchanged into every coil, because conductor architecture, stabilizer, insulation, no-insulation current paths, preload, field, temperature, and extraction topology change the observable signatures and safe thresholds. The scalable core is the acquisition hardware, firmware, analysis pipeline, test protocol, and traceability system; applications engineering remains substantial.

## Physics and build path

REBCO enables high fields and high current density, but its quench behavior is not a simple extension of mature low-temperature-superconductor practice. Reviews of REBCO for tokamak magnets and alternative fusion conductors support the enabling role of the material while documenting the engineering trade space ([L03-001](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6668/ada9d2), [L03-002](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/pop/article/30/12/120604/2930490/Prospects-of-an-alternative-superconductor)). Large-volume wire delivery to a commercial fusion customer shows that conductor manufacturing is already relevant to real programs, not just coupon science ([L03-003](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-81559-z)). At the same time, critical-current dependence on field, angle, temperature, and strain makes a localized disturbance difficult to infer from a single global electrical signal.

The sensing hypothesis is that distributed optical strain/temperature observations and fast electromagnetic channels expose a precursor before terminal voltage alone crosses its protection threshold. Rayleigh-backscatter measurements can, in principle, localize changes along the fiber; RF/acoustic channels can capture abrupt mechanical or electromagnetic events; voltage taps remain a necessary independent channel. The FPGA does not “predict quench” by opaque inference. It aligns synchronized signals, applies bounded filters and thresholds, checks channel health, and asserts a deterministic trip condition whose latency can be measured.

The protection half must remain coil specific. Upon a trip, the controller may open or commutate the supply, command an extraction switch, and trigger the host’s approved energy-spreading method. Modeling literature for HTS magnet protection and earlier detection designs establish relevant precedents ([L03-005](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9454326/), [L03-006](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10811977/), [L03-007](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7001571)). These precedents do not validate kilometer-scale fiber, multi-kA isolation, or a 100-MJ system. Those are scale-up questions that require staged hardware evidence.

The build sequence is therefore: instrument a small double-pancake; establish calibration stability through thermal and current cycling; seed controlled disturbances; compare sensor combinations against voltage-only detection; close the loop to a low-energy dump; then progress to a representative multi-kA coil only with a magnet partner. A high-field record in China confirms that fully superconducting magnet engineering continues to advance ([L03-029](https://www.the-innovation.org/article/id/695a0b800989772a128c5422)), but it does not reduce the US partner-access requirement.

## Named incumbents, substitutes, and the strongest bear case

The most important incumbents are not merchant startups. They are CFS’s internal magnet and protection teams, TE Magnetics’ internal engineering organization, and national-lab protection groups. CFS’s external magnet business and TE Magnetics’ dedicated commercialization effort demonstrate exactly why each could integrate protection rather than buy it ([L03-035](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/02/commonwealth-fusion-systems-leans-on-magnets-for-near-term-revenue/), [L03-033](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tokamak-energy-raises-125m-to-commercialize-transformative-fusion-and-magnet-technologies-302310607.html)). CERN’s CLIQ lineage is a named technical substitute for active quench spreading, though the concept’s differentiation is HTS-focused multi-modal detection plus qualification rather than a cosmetic repackage of LTS protection. No-insulation winding is the more existential substitute: passive current-sharing can make a separate fast-detection product less valuable, albeit with charging, control, and architecture tradeoffs. Component sensor and data-acquisition vendors can also move upward if a buyer supplies the coil knowledge.

The bear case is that quench protection is inseparable from magnet design, so no durable merchant socket exists. A builder that controls conductor, winding, joints, insulation, cryogenics, power supply, and safety analysis will not outsource the one subsystem that can destroy the asset. Each installation becomes custom engineering; qualification takes years; insurance and contractual liability consume the gross margin; and the buyer count remains tiny. A successful small-coil demonstration might establish an interesting instrument but fail to prove survivability, isolation, nuisance-trip performance, or safe extraction at production scale. Worse, no-insulation architectures could reduce the problem before a 2030 launch.

The investment answer is not to dismiss these objections. It is to use them as gates. No representative partner, no company. No separable interface specification, no product. No improvement over the buyer’s best voltage-only and no-insulation baselines, no 10x claim. No willingness to run the system in shadow mode on a larger coil, no scale-up.

## Explicit 10x test

“10x” means an order-of-magnitude increase in usable warning time relative to the same coil’s best tuned voltage-only baseline, without trading that gain for unacceptable nuisance trips. In the controlled campaign, each seeded event is time-aligned to a reference thermal-runaway criterion. The primary metric is warning interval from asserted protection command to that criterion. The absolute product threshold remains at least 100 ms median warning, below 1% false triggers across non-quench transients, zero missed thermal-runaway events, and sub-millisecond processing latency from validated threshold crossing to command output.

The 10x claim passes only if the multi-modal system produces at least ten times the median warning interval of the voltage-only baseline on the same blinded event set, preserves that advantage across current-ramp and cryogenic conditions, and does not rely on post hoc threshold tuning. No-insulation is tested as an architectural comparator rather than forced into the same latency metric: the partner must quantify whether passive current sharing already keeps hotspot temperature and voltage within its safe envelope. If it does, the merchant detection thesis may be unnecessary even when the sensors work.

## First experiment, budget, and pass/kill criteria

The frozen 2027 experiment is a 1–2 T REBCO double-pancake campaign at a university magnet lab. Co-wind the optical fiber, install RF pickup and voltage/temperature references, drive at least 50 seeded disturbances and 20 controlled quenches, and close the trigger to a bounded extraction circuit. The all-in budget stays at **$250,000**: approximately $65,000 for coil, conductor, winding, and fixtures; $55,000 for optical and electrical acquisition; $45,000 for isolated switching and dump hardware; $45,000 for cryogenic facility time and partner labor; $20,000 for safety review and test readiness; and $20,000 contingency. These are planning assumptions, not vendor quotes.

Pass requires at least 100 ms median warning before the defined runaway criterion, zero missed runaway events, fewer than 1% false triggers over the full transient set, sub-millisecond command initiation, and less than 5% calibration drift after cycling. It must also demonstrate the explicit 10x comparison above and reproduce the result in a held-out sequence that the algorithm team did not label in advance. A pass earns a partner-scale design review; it does not validate a production magnet.

Kill the concept if any runaway is missed, false trips exceed 1%, median warning is below 100 ms, the optical calibration drifts more than 5%, electrical isolation corrupts acquisition, or the result disappears under blinded replay. Also kill the company path if no US magnet builder offers a documented route to a representative multi-kA test after seeing the data. That last criterion separates good laboratory physics from a reachable business.

## Bottom-up niche arithmetic

This is a small project market. The only firm cohort anchor is the eight-company DOE milestone program ([L03-032](https://www.energy.gov/articles/us-department-energy-announces-selectees-107-million-fusion-innovation-research-engine)). Assume, explicitly, that four to six of those companies pursue an HTS-magnet program requiring external qualification between 2030 and 2034, and that each creates one or two addressable protection packages. Add two to four US lab or university programs. That yields **6–16 project packages** over five years.

Assume $2–5 million of revenue per package for instrumented sensing, isolated control/dump interfaces, qualification campaigns, and dossier support. The resulting cumulative niche is **$12–80 million**, with an illustrative midpoint of 11 packages at $3.5 million, or $38.5 million. If installed systems later generate $0.2–0.6 million per year in recalibration, configuration control, and event-analysis revenue, a 6–16-system base yields roughly **$1.2–9.6 million annual recurring revenue**. None of these prices is sourced from an existing merchant product; they are scenario assumptions to be replaced with partner budgets.

The arithmetic is adequate for a specialized scientific-systems company but not automatically venture-scale. Expansion to other superconducting assets should receive no valuation credit until the fusion wedge establishes a common interface and reusable qualification evidence.

## US route

**2026:** interview magnet protection leads, laboratory safety owners, and program managers; obtain written interface requirements without implying procurement; design a shadow-mode acquisition unit and controlled-quench protocol. **2027:** run the $250,000 double-pancake campaign and publish latency, miss-rate, nuisance-trip, and calibration results. **2028:** pursue DOE SBIR/INFUSE-class support and a joint test plan with one named magnet organization; conduct design assurance for isolation, extraction voltage, failure containment, and data traceability. **2029:** demonstrate on a representative multi-kA coil at a partner facility, first in shadow mode and then, only after review, with a bounded protection action; freeze v1 architecture and qualification plan.

The 2030 company launch is conditional on that sequence, not on the calendar alone. Initial sales are engineering and qualification programs. A later hardware release is possible only after interfaces stabilize across at least two installations. The route does not assume current partner access, a signed evaluation, or a purchase order.

## China route: separately assessed and not countable

China is **not a beachhead, not a secondary revenue case, and not included in the niche arithmetic**. The accepted evidence shows technically significant high-field magnet activity ([L03-029](https://www.the-innovation.org/article/id/695a0b800989772a128c5422)) and a superconducting-cable demonstration ([L03-012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772830722000072)), but neither is evidence that a China buyer will purchase this US subsystem. The base case contains no China sale, technical-data transfer, service route, or licensing revenue. Any future licensing discussion would be hypothetical, require current export counsel and counterparty screening, and remain outside the investment case unless separately approved. This preserves the frozen G6 pass-marginal verdict and `china_beachhead: false` flag.

## Safety, compliance, and liability

No dedicated certification standard for this exact HTS protection package was verified in the accepted evidence. Qualification must therefore be customer- and facility-specific. The minimum dossier needs requirements traceability, sensor calibration records, electrical-isolation tests, EMC testing, thermal and current-cycle history, firmware configuration control, fault-injection results, false-trip statistics, and an independent failure-mode and fault-tree review. The controller should default safe on loss of power or sensor health, retain hardwired independent overcurrent/overvoltage protection, and expose deterministic logs. It should begin as a shadow channel, never the sole line of defense.

Contractually, the vendor must define responsibility boundaries around coil design, extraction sizing, allowable voltage, threshold approval, and maintenance. Product liability, professional liability, incident investigation, and warranty exclusions should be priced before the representative-coil test. If a buyer demands full-system performance liability while withholding coil models or operating data, that is a commercial kill signal.

## 2030 launch and 2030–2034 triggers

The timing contract has two independent legs. First, the DOE milestone program’s post-FY2027 funding and renewal decisions will clarify which private programs progress toward pilot-scale hardware ([L03-032](https://www.energy.gov/articles/us-department-energy-announces-selectees-107-million-fusion-innovation-research-engine)). Second, SPARC’s physical magnet installation and the public early-2030s ARC target create a company-level scale-up clock ([P3R2-D-01-S01](https://www.prismnews.com/hobbies/nuclear-reactions/commonwealth-fusion-systems-installs-first-sparc-magnet-eyes-2026-plasma-milestone), [L03-035](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/02/commonwealth-fusion-systems-leans-on-magnets-for-near-term-revenue/)). National-lab magnet delivery provides a separate scientific-system channel rather than relying on one private program ([L03-030](https://www.iter.org/node/20687/us-iter-completes-central-solenoid-procurement-package)).

Launch in 2030 only if precompany work has produced a reproducible small-coil result, a representative-coil access path, an approved safety boundary, and evidence that at least one buyer views the subsystem as separable. From 2030–2034, refresh funding awards, magnet construction schedules, winding architecture choices, and actual protection procurement every six months. Kill commercial readiness by December 2033 if no paid qualification program or written design-in exists.

## Risks and kill rules

The principal risks are no merchant socket, no-insulation substitution, scale failure from a small coil to multi-kA/high-energy systems, fiber debonding or calibration drift, nuisance trips, isolation failure, opaque buyer data, funding delay, long qualification, and catastrophic-liability exposure. Kill immediately on a missed controlled runaway, non-repeatable warning advantage, or a partner conclusion that protection cannot be separated from proprietary magnet design. Pause scale-up if energy-extraction voltage or insulation requirements exceed the bounded architecture. Kill by end-2029 if no representative US access path exists; kill by end-2033 if no paid qualification or design-in exists.

## Evidence durability versus refresh-sensitive claims

The durable evidence is the peer-reviewed physics: HTS quench-detection difficulty, REBCO strain and delamination sensitivity, critical-current behavior, protection modeling, and the continued technical relevance of REBCO to high-field magnets ([L03-004](https://doi.org/10.3390/instruments5030027), [L03-018](https://academic.oup.com/nsr/article/10/3/nwad001/6973212), [L03-005](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9454326/), [L03-006](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10811977/)). Refresh-sensitive claims include program appropriations, SPARC and ARC dates, the number of active builders, CFS’s external transactions, TE Magnetics’ strategy, partner willingness, unit pricing, and all procurement assumptions. Those items must be rechecked before the 2027 experiment and at least semiannually thereafter. Academic feasibility should not be confused with a current commercial socket.

## Verdict

**Advance as a partner-gated precompany experiment; do not underwrite it yet as a merchant catalog business.** The physics problem is real, the experiment is decisive at small-coil scale, and the US timing window is plausible. The 62.8 score and low confidence are appropriate because product-specific demand, representative access, full-scale protection, and buy-versus-build behavior remain unresolved. The next irreversible spend is justified only after a named US magnet organization accepts the test interface and liability boundary. China remains non-countable. The idea survives because it can be killed cleanly by data and partner behavior before company formation—not because the evidence already proves a market.
