# Source and claim policy

- Prefer standards bodies, regulators, government laboratories, peer-reviewed
  papers, official technical documentation, original datasets, company filings,
  and manufacturer data sheets.
- Use current web research for market, regulation, product, pricing, company,
  standards, and technology-status claims that may have changed.
- Every material current claim needs a source mapping with URL, publisher,
  publication date when available, access date, and the exact claim supported.
- Search snippets are discovery aids, not final evidence. Open the underlying
  source.
- The Operation B literature core accepts only verified peer-reviewed journal
  research or review articles. Peer-reviewed conference papers may be retained
  as supplementary evidence when the specific venue process is verified.
  Preprints, theses, patents, magazines, vendor material, and non-peer-reviewed
  reports remain discovery or contextual evidence and must be labeled.
- Verify DOI/title/year against the publisher record, de-duplicate normalized
  DOIs, and check corrections, expressions of concern, and retractions before
  accepting a publication. A DOI or publisher brand alone does not prove peer
  review.
- For measurement papers, extract experimental conditions, calibration
  traceability, uncertainty, repeatability/reproducibility, controls,
  limitations, and applicability to the proposed operating regime.
- Use PRISMA-inspired search/selection reporting for transparency, but do not
  claim a formal systematic review or meta-analysis unless its full methods
  were actually performed.
- Mark paywalled, inaccessible, stale, secondary, or geographically limited
  evidence. Do not silently generalize.
- Separate direct evidence from inference. Give confidence and a falsifier for
  consequential inferences.
- Patent and freedom-to-operate work is strategic screening, not legal advice.
  Safety, certification, and research planning are not professional approvals.
- Never fabricate a citation, quote, DOI, standard number, regulation, market
  size, competitor, customer, or experimental result.
